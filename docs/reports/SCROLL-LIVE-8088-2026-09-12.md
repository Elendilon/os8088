# Could anything scroll LIVE on a 4.77 MHz 8088? Four bars, three hand speeds, the rate swept

**Taken 2026-09-12** on `elendilon-next` at `0d3219c`, in an Ubuntu container.
**Instrument:** MartyPC (`make marty`), machines `os8088_5150_cga_gla` (4.77 MHz
8088, CGA) and `os8088_xt_vga` (the same CPU with a register-level VGA).
Guest milliseconds are the emulator's own cycle count at 4.772727 MHz.

**This file is a MEASUREMENT and is true of that tree and no other.** It is not
maintained against a later one; a second reading is a new file.

## The question

SPEC.md 13.10.5.4.1 gave every scroll bar a rate for an 8086 and a rate for a
286, and shipped the 8086 column at **0** — the view waits for the release —
on the ground that one commit is 117 to 417 ms there
(`docs/reports/SBRATE-COMMIT-COST-2026-09-11.md`). That ground was measured at
**rate 0**, where a drag's single commit jumps the whole distance the hand
travelled. It says nothing about what a commit costs when it happens *nine
times a second and moves two rows*, which is the only question that decides
whether an 8088 can follow a thumb.

## Three things the harness had to get right first

Two earlier versions of this got each of them wrong, and each wrong version
produced a confident, meaningless table.

1. **A host round trip costs 17.1 GUEST ms here.** Measured three ways — 40
   mouse packets, 40 `status()` calls, 40 two-byte reads — all 17.1. So the
   harness paces the hand, not the script's intent. Driving the drag through
   the ABSOLUTE mouse driver, which confirms every packet by reading guest
   memory, crossed the track in **24 guest seconds**: ~50x slower than a
   person, and every rate came back with zero lag because the throttle never
   had to refuse anything.
2. **...and bare back-to-back packets are too FAST.** 17.1 ms is under the
   1200-baud UART's own 25 ms floor, so such a stream is dropped. Exactly two
   round trips per packet — the send and one view read — is 34.2 ms, which is
   **29 packets a second** against a real serial mouse's 40. That is the
   fastest faithful hand available here and it is the one used; every reading
   below is therefore against a hand ~1.4x gentler than the worst a person can
   produce.
3. **So the hand's position is computed, not read.** A third round trip would
   take the pace to 51 ms and make the hand unrealistically slow, so
   `os88ui_sbpos`'s inverse (13.10.5.3) is mirrored on the host. The VIEW is
   what is sampled, which also makes the commit count exact rather than
   inferred.

**No knob tree was built.** The rate is poked into `os88ui_sbd_rate` after the
grab: `os88ui_sbgrab` writes that byte at the press and nothing reads it again
until the first `W_ONDRAG`. A package's copy of it is its own — the element is
compiled into the image — and `tests/dispapps.py`'s package map reaches it.

**`lag` is in ROWS**, |hand − view| sampled between packets. It is the number
that decides the question: a bar whose lag stays near zero is following the
hand, and one whose lag is a windowful is a slideshow.

## Five findings

**1. A rate is only a throttle while the commit is FASTER than the window.**
Note Pad is the proof and it is unambiguous: 3 commits at rate 1 and 3 at rate
6, identical, because one commit is 360 ms — 6.6 ticks — so even the lowest
window of 55 ms is already spent before the commit returns. Past that point
the rate is inert and the bar runs flat out. **This corrects 13.10.5.4.1's own
framing**, which treats "the throttle becomes a no-op" as the failure mode of
too small a rate. It is not a failure mode; it is simply the rate ceasing to
be the binding quantity.

**2. Where the rate IS a throttle, LOWER is better on an 8088** — monotonically,
on every bar and at every hand speed. Disk window, 1.2 s hand: 1.4 rows of lag
at rate 1 against 4.0 at rate 6. The cause is a feedback the rate controls: a
shorter window means a smaller delta per commit, which means fewer exposed rows
to letter and, on three of the four bars, the `gfx_scroll` tier instead of a
repaint. A longer window buys nothing and pays for it twice.

**3. Rate 0 is the worst setting on every bar, by construction.** Its mean lag
is half the travel — 15 rows of 26, 41 of 69, 106 of 185 — because the view is
where the drag started until the button comes up. It is not a conservative
choice on the performance scale; it is the absence of one.

**4. ROWS PER PIXEL decides whether a bar can reach its own fast path**, and it
is a property of the DOCUMENT, not the window. Disk window 0.40, dialog 0.38,
Browser 0.91, **Note Pad 2.18**. Note Pad's blit needs the hand to move under
`np_vrows` = 16 rows between commits, which is 7.4 px; at 360 ms a commit and a
1.2-second crossing of 93 px the hand moves **31 px**. It is structurally
locked out of its own fast path — and would not be on a 60-line document,
where the same thumb would be ~0.5 rows a pixel.

**5. `fit` barely moves the commit at a live rate.** CGA `fit` 5 is 78 ms and
VGA `fit` 8 is 84. Only the delta's rows are relettered, so the window's height
reaches the cost through the blit's band alone — and `gfx_scroll` is 227 µs a
pixel row on VGA against 400 on mono (PERFORMANCE.md Set 54), which is why the
planar adapter with the taller window came out level. **A MAXIMISED window was
not measured**: arithmetically a 28-row VGA Disk window adds ~320 pixel rows of
blit, ~96 ms, for ~180 ms a commit — which would still track, and against a
28-row windowful the proportional lag would be *better* than the small
window's. That is an estimate, not a reading.

## The verdicts

| bar | commit | live on an 8088? | best rate |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **Disk window** | 78–84 ms | **YES** — 1 row behind at a normal drag, on CGA and VGA alike | **1** |
| **Standard File dialog** | 169–179 ms | **YES** — 1–2 rows behind, with no fast path at all | **1 or 2** (they measure identically) |
| **Browser** | 130–210 ms | **PARTLY** — a deliberate drag tracks at 2 rows; a 1.2 s sweep is 9 behind | 2 |
| **Note Pad** | ~360 ms | **NO, at any rate** — and the rate is not the lever | — |

**The file manager is the answer to the question as asked**, and it is not
marginal: at rate 1 a normal drag keeps the view within one row of the hand on
both adapters, and the reason is that `fm_scroll_by` already has the blit tier
and a live rate is what keeps deltas small enough to stay on it.

## What this would change, if taken

`FM_SBRATE` 0 → **1** and `FD_SBRATE` 0 → **1**, in `kernel/files.inc` and
`kernel/fdlg.inc`. The package column (`SB_RATE`) stays 0: the Browser is the
only package that would benefit and it benefits only on a slow hand.

**Both bars would then be effectively unthrottled on an 8088** — 55 ms is under
either commit cost — so rate 1 there means "commit on every `W_ONDRAG`". The
measurement endorses that rather than merely tolerating it: lag stays at 1 row,
and the view had reached the hand *before* the release in every rate-1 arm.
Nothing else on the machine suffers for it, because the pointer is ISR-paced
(SPEC.md 9.10) and other tasks are preempted by IRQ0 regardless.
