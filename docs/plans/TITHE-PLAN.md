# TITHE-PLAN.md — a two-player turn-based card duel for os8088

**STATUS: PLAN, REVISION 3. Nothing is built.** Every number below that is not
marked *measured* is an **estimate**, and §3.7 is the bench that has to run
before the renderer's shape is fixed or a pixel of art is drawn.

This file is the design record. When work lands, SPEC.md gains a section of its
own — the next free number is **97** — which becomes the binding contract, and
this file moves to `docs/plans/completed/`.

**Revision 3 settles the five decisions that make TITHE a game of pure
placement.** Turn order **alternates each round**, which removes the
information advantage structurally and with it every resource compensation.
**Nothing is chosen while a round resolves**: no activated abilities, no
targeting prompts, no reactions and no randomness — every choice is made on
your own turn and becomes part of the board, which is what a **STANCE** is
(§5.4). **Healing fires inside the combat phase, on the healer's own lane,
after that lane's attacks**, which makes a healer's lane a third placement
dimension and lets it pull a character back from zero. And **front and rear may
be entirely different roles** — a tank in front and a farmer in the rear, the
same person holding a different tool — which doubles the character art and is
worth it.

## 0. The ask

| | |
|---|---|
| **the game** | **TITHE** — package stem `TITHE`, file `TITHE.O88` |
| players | 2 — hot-seat, AI, or networked |
| network | two os8088 machines over Ethernet; **and** two machines on a direct null-modem cable (serial or parallel) |
| solo | an AI opponent, and a **campaign** — a map of AI duels that earns cards |
| deck | built from cards you own, **one faction per deck**; three factions at launch, room for more |
| board | **4 columns × 5 lanes**, **isometric**, 10 characters a side |
| resources | **GOLD** (generated by characters, mostly in the rear) and **SOULS** (earned when an enemy dies, by its power rating) |
| presentation | windowed **or** fullscreen; animated front menu with custom graphical buttons |
| animation | **23 animated features** at once — 20 characters, 2 player bases, 1 moused-over card |
| framerate | **18 fps, held, always** |
| disk | **350KB for the whole game** — it ships on a 360KB floppy of its own |
| heap | must run in **~400KB of free heap**, holding at most two factions' art |

| faction | focus | weakness |
|---|---|---|
| **THE BULWARK** | shields, gold generation, melee — the straightforward faction | reach; nothing answers a board it cannot walk to |
| **THE EMBER CHOIR** | ranged damage, paid for in **souls** | gold generation and melee |
| **THE COVENANT** | positional specials and **healing** | raw damage; it wins by not losing |

**Every faction carries a full set of roles** — melee, ranged, shielding and
generation specialists, and combinations — and its focus is where its *best*
cards and its keywords live, never the only thing it can do (§7.1).

## 1. The envelope — the arithmetic everything else obeys

This section is first on purpose. **18 fps with 23 animated features on a
4.77 MHz 8088 is the hardest constraint in the brief, and it decides the art
format, the sprite size and the animation rate.**

### 1.1 The measured constants

| | |
|---|---|
| system tick | **18.2065 Hz** → **54.925 ms** a frame |
| the target machine | IBM PC/XT, **8088 at 4.77 MHz** → ~262,000 cycles a frame |
| any `gfx_*` arrival, small call | **~756 µs** *measured*, and ~20% less since §5.7 — see the warning |
| `OSAPI_GFX_BLIT1`, 128×128 band | **12,588 µs** *measured* (PERFORMANCE.md Set 77) |
| ...so a 1bpp band costs | **6.15 µs a byte**, **0.77 µs a pixel** *derived* |
| `OSAPI_GFX_BLIT4`, VGA planar decoder | **106.9 cycles a pixel** = **22.4 µs a pixel** *measured* |
| `OSAPI_GFX_BLITP`, four planes | *"the bytes and nothing else"* — **estimate ~3.1 µs a pixel**. **NOT MEASURED anywhere in this tree. §3.7 measures it.** |
| RAM `rep stosw` | **1.76 µs a byte** *measured* |
| RAM byte read-modify-write (a 5-instruction loop) | **15.3 µs a byte** *measured* |
| a full task switch | 693 µs *measured* |

> **Do not quote 756 µs as a floor a design must beat.** PERFORMANCE.md Part 2
> says so in as many words: it is the fixed part of a *small* call, `gfx_hline`
> is 678 µs for 6 pixels and 862 for 312, and Set 89 is the worked example of a
> design argued from the aggregate and got wrong. What this plan leans on is
> the **per-byte band cost**, because the renderer is one blit a feature.

### 1.2 The finding that decides the art format

Three ways to put one 72×64 character on a VGA:

| route | bytes moved | cost *(est.)* | 23 features, one update each |
|---|---|---|---|
| `GFX_BLIT4` — packed 4bpp, kernel decodes | — | 4,608 px × 22.4 µs = **103.2 ms** | **2.4 seconds.** Unusable. |
| `GFX_BLITP` — 4 planes, pre-composed | 2,304 | **~14.2 ms** | **326 ms** → 3.1 rounds a second |
| `GFX_BLIT1` + pen — 1bpp band, two colours | 576 | **~3.54 ms** | **81.4 ms** → 12.3 rounds a second |

**`gfx_blit4` is out for anything that moves.** One character would be two
whole frames. It is the right slot for a picture drawn once and the wrong one
for animation.

**Sixteen colours costs 4× a 1bpp band, and no trick avoids it**: 16 colours
*is* four bits a pixel, and `gfx_blitp` is already a bare `rep movsb` per plane
per row with zero transpose. It is the floor for full colour, and the floor is
four times the one-bit floor.

**So the sprite is a 1bpp band with `OSAPI_GFX_BLIT1_PEN`'s two colours**
(§3.5), with richer arms for machines that can pay.

### 1.3 …and so the animation rate is a budget

Take **40% of the frame** for idle animation (22 ms), leaving 60% for the HUD,
the card panel, input, the AI's background thinking and slack. With 23
features:

| surface | sprite band | bytes | per commit | commits/frame at 40% | **fps a feature** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| VGA fullscreen 640×480 | 72×64 | 576 | 3.54 ms | 6.2 | **4.9** |
| VGA windowed | 56×48 | 336 | 2.07 ms | 10.6 | **8.4** |
| Hercules 720×348 | 80×46 | 460 | 2.83 ms | 7.8 | **6.2** |
| CGA 640×200 | 56×26 | 182 | 1.12 ms | 19.6 | **15.6** |
| *(VGA fullscreen, four planes)* | 72×64 | 2,304 | 14.2 ms | 1.5 | **1.2** |

**A 4-frame ping-pong idle (A B C B) at 4.9 fps is a 0.8-second cycle** — a
slow breath, which is what a crowd of twenty figures should do.

**A 386 is a different machine and gets a different answer.**
`OSAPI_CPU_INFO` answers `CPU_8086 / CPU_286 / CPU_386` — *a fact the code can
test* rather than a guess about speed (PERFORMANCE.md rule 7) — and §6.9 turns
that into a **Detail** setting with a measured default.

### 1.4 The three adapters

| adapter | screen | pixel aspect | what the board gets |
|---|---|---|---|
| VGA | 640×480, 16 colours | square | the design target |
| Hercules | 720×348, 1bpp | ~square | wider, shorter. Workable |
| CGA | **640×200**, 1bpp | **~1 : 2.4** | only possible because the cards moved right (§3.3) |

**CGA's non-square pixel is what saves it.** A 56×26 sprite displays at roughly
56 × 62 *effective* pixels, so a short sprite is not a squashed one — it is a
correctly-proportioned figure on a raster that is short in rows. `ddlay.inc`
(§93.3) already reads the adapter's pixel aspect for exactly this reason, and
the cut is therefore **per axis** (§4.2).

**A 1bpp adapter reads on one bit and the pen is NOT READ there** (§5.4.2.2).
Faction colour is a VGA feature, and the art must read as **silhouette plus
interior line** on the mono adapters — §93.2.1's finding one game along.

### 1.5 Disk and heap

**The disk is 354 clusters of 1,024 bytes** — a 360KB floppy's real capacity.
Every package and data file on a shipped disk is **lz4-compressed** by default
(`PKGZ ?= lz4`, §20.13), so byte counts must be measured against the
*compressed* size; pixel art compresses well and code does not.

**Two poses a character (§4.2.1) doubles the largest line**, and it still fits:

| | uncompressed | on disk (est. ×0.6) |
|---|---|---|
| the package primary (`.O88`) | ~52KB | ~32KB |
| **character sprites — 48 × 2 poses × 4 frames × 576 B** | **221KB** | **~110KB** |
| card faces — 48 × art + frame | ~30KB | ~15KB |
| player bases — 3 factions × 6 frames | 27KB | ~14KB |
| board art — 3 terrains, isometric, static | 60KB | ~25KB |
| menu art, buttons, title | ~40KB | ~20KB |
| campaign map | ~30KB | ~15KB |
| **total** | | **~231KB of 354** |

**123 clusters spare**, and §4.2.2 names a pipeline change that buys ~60KB more
if it is ever needed. **The disk is not the binding constraint. The frame is.**

**Heap**: at most two factions are live, and **both poses of every card must be
resident**, because a swap flips a character's pose instantly and cannot wait
for a disk. 2 factions × 16 cards × 2 poses × 4 frames × 576 B = **147KB** of
sprites, plus the board, the HUD, the card panel and the composition scratch —
call it **220KB** against ~400KB free. Comfortable, and comfortable *because*
the sprites are 1bpp: the four-plane variant is 590KB of sprites alone.

### 1.6 What TITHE is not

- **Not a `kern_small` package.** It wants ~220KB of heap and a 128KB machine
  has ~50KB. It goes in `$(SMALLOMIT)` with a stated reason, and
  `soak -k 'smallreq'` is the gate that keeps it there (§24.5).
- **Not an `fsx` bracket with a foreign mode.** It is turn-based and wants the
  kernel's cursor, the event ladder and the file API. §11.2's fullscreen
  *surface* — a real window covering the screen — is what fullscreen means
  here, and **windowed and fullscreen are one renderer at two sizes** (§3.1).

## 2. The board

```
        BULWARK side                      EMBER CHOIR side
     rear      front                   front       rear
  +---------+---------+   .   .   +---------+---------+
  | (0,0)   | (1,0)   |           | (2,0)   | (3,0)   |    lane 0   ^ resolves
  +---------+---------+           +---------+---------+             | first
  | (0,1)   | (1,1)   |           | (2,1)   | (3,1)   |    lane 1   |
  ...                                                      lanes 2, 3, 4
```

- **4 columns × 5 lanes.** Column 0 = P1 rear, 1 = P1 front, 2 = P2 front,
  3 = P2 rear. **A "lane" runs across all four columns.**
- **10 characters a side, maximum** — the reference screenshot's `4/10`.
- The players' **bases** sit outside the grid and are two of the 23 animated
  features.
- **Attacks travel straight across a lane**, and only during the combat phase.
- **Lanes resolve top to bottom, 0 → 4**, and that ordering is load-bearing:
  it is what gives a healer's lane meaning (§5.5.2).

## 3. The renderer

### 3.1 One surface, sized from the content box

`DOT DELIRIUM` is the precedent and its three consequences are ours (§93): it
is **bigger on a Hercules than on a CGA**, it draws **one blit an actor a frame
and nothing else**, and it **re-measures the surface** rather than assuming one.

- `OSAPI_WM_PREFER` takes exactly three (w, h) pairs, in VGA / HERC / CGA order.
- `OSAPI_WM_OWNBG` is set — we paint every pixel of our content, so the
  kernel's white fill in front of `W_PAINT` is skipped. Without it every repaint
  flashes white first, which is PERFORMANCE.md rule 2's canonical violation.
- `OSAPI_WM_NOANIM` — a zoom-open on a 450-pixel window is a second of nothing.
- Fullscreen is `OSAPI_FULLSCREEN`. §11.2.1 binds the keys: **F** toggles,
  **Esc** leaves. The layout recomputes and the sprite masters are re-cut.
- `OSAPI_WM_ONRESIZE` re-runs the layout when the content box changed and we
  did not ask.

**Layout is a table computed once per size change**, never per frame. A resize
is not a thing that happens inside a frame — §93.5's own words.

### 3.2 ISOMETRIC — the shear that costs nothing

The requirement is an isometric board where each character is fully inside its
own space and never overlaps another. Those two together have one clean answer:
the cell grid is **sheared**, not projected.

```
  screen x of cell (c, r) = BX + c * CW
  screen y of cell (c, r) = BY + r * CH - c * RISE
```

- Two cells in the same column are `CH` apart vertically → no overlap.
- Two cells in adjacent columns are `CW` apart horizontally → no overlap.
- **The rectangles tile exactly. Non-overlap is a property of the layout, not a
  promise the art has to keep.**
- `CW` is a **multiple of 8**, so every cell's x satisfies `gfx_blit1`'s one
  alignment rule for free (§5.4.2 refusal 1). `BX` is 8-aligned by §11.94's
  content-origin snap, which is the default.
- A **lane** therefore runs up-and-to-the-right across the screen — the
  isometric "across" axis, and the direction an attack travels. The reference
  screenshot's units form exactly those up-right lines.

**The isometric read comes from the art, not the geometry**: the static board is
painted in perspective (ground plane, water, fences, each player's territory and
buildings), each cell carries a baked isometric ground diamond, characters are
drawn three-quarter view with a drop shadow, and the column rise does the rest.

**This is what makes isometric free.** A projected isometric would mean
overlapping bounding boxes, a painter's draw order and non-rectangular bands —
and a band that is not a rectangle is not something `gfx_blit1` takes.

**One consequence for the art**: the four combat columns sit on **one shared
ground texture**, with the cell pitch a whole number of ground tiles, so every
cell's baked ground is the identical diamond. If ground varied per cell a sprite
frame would need one baked variant per cell and the art would multiply by
twenty. The fiction already agrees: each player's terrain, fence, buildings and
base live **behind** their rear column, outside the grid, exactly as the
reference has them.

#### 3.2.1 Starting geometry

Computed at layout time; these are the values the layout math should produce,
not constants to hard-code.

| surface | HUD | card panel | `CW` × `CH` | `RISE` | board box | sprite band |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| VGA 640×480 full | 36 | 176 | **104 × 72** | 20 | 416 × 420 | **72 × 64** |
| VGA windowed | 28 | 144 | 80 × 56 | 16 | 320 × 328 | 56 × 48 |
| Hercules 720×348 | 32 | 168 | 120 × 52 | 16 | 480 × 308 | 80 × 46 |
| CGA 640×200 | 24 | 160 | 80 × 30 | 8 | 320 × 174 | 56 × 26 |

### 3.3 The card panel is on the right, and that is what rescues CGA

Cards live in a **vertical strip down the right-hand edge**, not along the
bottom. Every row of board height the hand does not take is a row the five lanes
get, and on a 640×200 CGA that is the difference between a board and a refusal.

The panel holds the hand (up to 7 cards as a vertical list, the moused-over one
expanded and animated — the 23rd feature), the resource readouts, the **swaps
remaining** counter and the **End Turn** button. Same shape on all three
adapters, so one layout body rather than three.

### 3.4 One blit a feature, and the ground is baked into it

**The rule, and it is the whole renderer**: a character is put down as **one
opaque band that includes its own ground**, so drawing it where it used to be
*is* the erase. There is no instant at which a sprite is off the glass, no
second call to be pre-empted between, and no erase pass at all.

This is §79.5.1's finding and §93.5.1's. The screen saver's first draft blanked
the box and then drew six primitives into it; the field reported *"the fish
flicker"*, because 4.5 ms with the sprite absent is not a brightness dip, it is
a strobe. **We do not get to relearn this.**

**THE BAND IS THE SPRITE'S OWN BOX, NOT THE CELL.** The cell is 104×72 and the
sprite band is 72×64 — the rest of the cell is static ground, drawn once with
the board and never touched again. That is 576 bytes a commit instead of 936,
and it is worth **38% of the animation budget** for one line of layout
arithmetic.

**And a band is cut on the CELL grid, not the byte grid.** `gfx_blit1` only asks
for a multiple of 8, and a band cut there reaches part-way into the next cell —
so whatever is drawn there is composed partly, which on the glass is a thing
that changes shape when a neighbour animates and changes back. §93.5.1 paid for
that one; cells are a multiple of 8 wide by construction (§3.2), so cutting on
the cell grid costs nothing.

### 3.5 Colour — the pen, and the strip trick

`OSAPI_GFX_BLIT1_PEN` (slot 0x04A8) says what a set bit and a clear bit become:
**two colours a band, in one pass, for free** — measured at +0.62% on a 128×128
band, a fixed ~78 µs (§5.4.2.2). On a 1bpp adapter the pen is *ignored rather
than refused*, so one body runs everywhere.

Three arms, in increasing cost:

- **(a) `Flat` — one pen a character.** Twenty characters are twenty two-tone
  figures in up to twenty colour pairs. Cheapest.
- **(b) `Banded` — horizontal strips, one pen each.** The sprite is blitted as
  2–3 stacked bands — head, body, base — each with its own pen. **The bytes are
  identical**; what is added is one arrival per extra strip. Buys 4–6 colours a
  character at close to 1bpp cost. **The likely default on VGA**, and §3.7 must
  price it rather than this paragraph.
- **(c) `Rich` — four planes** via `gfx_blitp`, 16 colours, 4× the bytes. §1.3
  says ~1.2 fps a feature on an 8088 and perfectly affordable on a 386.

The art pipeline emits all three from one master (§4), so the setting costs disk
and not drawing time.

### 3.6 The pacing wheel — 23 features at a fixed rate

**A fixed budget, not a hope.** The renderer holds the 23 animated features and
a **credit in bytes per frame**, set at layout time from the measured per-byte
cost and the frame share. Each frame:

1. Walk the wheel from where it stopped last frame.
2. For each feature whose animation clock says it is due, commit its band and
   subtract its byte cost from the frame's credit.
3. Stop when the credit is spent. **Remember the position.**

- **A consistent animation speed**, because a feature's clock advances on the
  system tick and not on how many neighbours drew. A feature that misses its
  slot is *late by one frame*, never slowed.
- **Overrun is impossible by construction.** The credit is the contract.
- **No starvation**, because the wheel resumes rather than restarts.
- **Graceful degradation**: on an expensive surface every feature slows
  together, which an eye forgives. What it removes is the failure an eye does
  not forgive — some characters animating smoothly while others visibly hitch.
- **The combat phase takes the whole credit** (§3.6.1).

Idle clocks are **not** free-running per feature: features share a phase table
with a per-feature offset, so twenty characters do not all inhale on the same
tick. Twenty synchronised idles read as one pulsing blob; staggered ones read as
a crowd.

#### 3.6.1 The combat phase

Attacks resolve **simultaneously from both sides**, **≤1 second a lane**, and
the animation may focus solely on the lane being resolved. The idle wheel is
suspended and the whole frame budget goes to that lane: at most four attacking
characters, their projectiles, the impacts, the healing that follows them and
the floating numbers. Five lanes × ≤18 frames = **≤5 seconds**, and **Fast
Combat** (§6.9) halves it.

**Simultaneity is a resolution rule, not an animation one.** Both sides' damage
for a lane is computed from the state at the *start of that lane*, then applied
together — so two characters that would kill each other both die. The animation
plays them together; the arithmetic never interleaves.

### 3.7 WAVE 0 — the bench that decides all of the above

**Nothing in §3.2–§3.6 is settled until this has run**, and it is the first
thing built: a `tests/` package (never shipped, §19.10's rule) that puts up 23
bands of a configurable size and format and reports guest cycles a frame off
MartyPC's own counter.

| row | question |
|---|---|
| `blit1` at each of §3.2.1's four sprite sizes | the per-byte band cost on **this** build — §1.1's 6.15 µs is derived from a measurement of a *128×128* band and must be confirmed at ours |
| `blit1` + pen, same sizes | what the pen's fixed ~78 µs really is at a small band |
| `blit1` × 2 and × 3 strips | §3.5(b)'s extra arrivals, priced rather than estimated |
| `blitp` at two sizes | the four-plane floor — **this number does not exist anywhere in the tree** |
| every row on Hercules and CGA | the 1bpp adapters are not a scaled VGA |
| the full 23-feature wheel | the whole-frame figure, which is the only one that answers the brief |
| a part load + expand | §4.3's load path, where PAINT-1BPP-PLAN found its real regression |

Measured on **MartyPC** — a cycle-accurate 4.77 MHz 8088, the default
instrument — into a dated `docs/reports/` file, because a measurement is true of
the tree it was taken on and of no other. It also settles a question nothing
else can: **whether §3.2.1's cell sizes survive, or have to come down.**

## 4. Assets — one master, three outputs

### 4.1 The pipeline

Art is authored as PNG at **one master size per kind** and a host-side tool,
`tools/os88tithe.py` (in `os88logo.py`'s and `cp437font.py`'s shape), emits the
shipped blobs.

```
art/char/<faction>/<card>_<pose>_<frame>.png  ->  CHARS.DAT   (sprite bank)
art/card/<faction>/<card>.png                 ->  FACES.DAT   (card faces)
art/base/<faction>_<frame>.png                ->  BASES.DAT
art/board/<terrain>.png                       ->  BOARD.DAT
art/menu/*.png                                ->  MENU.DAT
art/map/*.png                                 ->  MAP.DAT
```

From one master the tool emits, per record: the **1bpp band** (threshold plus an
authored dither for the mono adapters), the **pen pair** or the **strip table**,
and — if `Rich` survives §3.7 — the **four-plane block**.

### 4.2 The cut is PER AXIS, and that is why one master works

Masters are authored at the **VGA-fullscreen** sprite size (72×64 for a
character) and **cut down to the live size at layout time by nearest
neighbour** — §93.5's approach, where a rebuild is "thirty-five images of at
most 32 bytes and happens when the window changes size, which is not a thing
that happens in a frame."

**x and y are cut by different factors**, driven by the adapter's pixel aspect
(§1.4). That is what lets one 72×64 master serve a 56×26 CGA sprite and still
produce a figure with correct proportions on the glass.

§93.5's lesson comes with the technique:

> **A scaled row is a fixed number of bytes whatever the cell is**, so a narrow
> image is a wide one with an empty tail. `dotdel` shipped a cutter that packed
> one byte a row while the composer strode two, and on a CGA every actor was
> noise. One constant, used by the writer and the reader.

**The CGA fallback, named and costed now**: if the CGA cut of a detailed
character is unacceptable on a real screen, a hand-tuned small master set for
**characters only** is 48 × 2 poses × 4 × 182 B = **70KB uncompressed**
(~42KB on disk). A decision taken in wave 5 after somebody looks at a CGA, not
before.

#### 4.2.1 TWO POSES A CHARACTER — the cost, and why it is worth it

**Front and rear are allowed to be entirely different roles** (§5.2): the same
person is a shield-and-sword tank in the front column and a pitchfork-and-scythe
farmer in the rear. So **every character has two sprite sets** — same face, same
build, same colours, different stance and different tool in hand.

| | |
|---|---|
| what it costs on disk | **+110KB uncompressed, ~+55KB packed** — the largest single line in §1.5, and it still leaves 123 clusters spare |
| what it costs in heap | **+74KB resident** during a match, because a swap flips a pose instantly and cannot wait for a disk |
| what it costs the frame | **nothing.** A pose is chosen when the band's source pointer is picked, which is a table lookup |
| what it buys | the placement decision is **which character this is**, not which numbers it has. That is the deepest thing in the game and it is cheap |

**The art rule that makes it readable**: the two poses must be recognisably the
same person. Same silhouette family, same palette pair, same head. What changes
is the stance, the tool and the idle motion — a tank shifts its weight behind a
shield, a farmer swings a scythe. On the two 1bpp adapters the **tool is the
tell**, because colour is gone there and the silhouette is all the player has.

#### 4.2.2 The pipeline change that buys 60KB, if it is ever needed

**Not proposed for revision 3** — §1.5 has 123 clusters spare — and recorded so
it is not re-derived under pressure: store each character once as a **body**
frame set plus two small **prop/stance overlays**, and composite the two
complete opaque poses at *load* time. Disk falls by roughly 60KB; heap does not
move; the cost is a masked RAM composite of ~256 records at 15.3 µs a byte,
about **0.4 s once per match**. Reach for it only if the disk gets tight.

### 4.3 Where the bytes live at run time

`apps/os88parts.inc` (§20.12): named, sized parts inside the one `.O88`, claimed
and loaded on demand, compressed on disk (`OP_COMP`) and optionally lazy
(`OP_LAZY`).

| part | flags | when |
|---|---|---|
| `MENU` | `OP_COMP`, `OP_LAZY` | at the front menu; dropped when a match starts |
| `BOARD` | `OP_COMP` | at match start |
| `FACTION` ×3 | `OP_COMP`, `OP_LAZY` | **only the two in play** — the line that makes 400KB work |
| `MAP` | `OP_COMP`, `OP_LAZY` | campaign only |

**Why parts and not sidecar files**: `apps/c64`'s ROMs were a sidecar until
§20.12 wave 6, and *"a copy that took the program and left it behind was a
machine that could not start."* One file is one file.

**The load path is the regression to watch.** PAINT-1BPP-PLAN is the design
record and SPEC.md §42.25 the contract; between them they record a decoder that
shipped at 199 cycles a pixel against the packed arm's 49 — the *load* half was
the real cost, not the draw half. §3.7 gives ours a number.

### 4.4 The heap, and the rule that bites

Every claim is born **pinned** (§66). The package's own region and every art
claim must declare `OS88_REGION_MOVABLE` with a relocation **proc**;
`tests/unit/t_movable.py` fails the build otherwise, and the way out is a line
in `tests/movable.txt` with a reason — a list that only turns one way.

**A region declaration alone is inert if the package hires a worker**: §66.6.2's
frame pins the region however it is declared, so the worker needs
`OS88_WORKER_RESTARTABLE` at the spawn. We hire one (§10.5, §12.2), so both are
owed.

Before declaring, ask what segment words we hold that point inside ourselves.
Here that is the **sprite bank index** — if it stores segments, the proc has real
work; if it stores offsets from a base, the proc is a `ret`. **Store offsets.**

## 5. The rules

### 5.0 THE FIRST PRINCIPLE — every choice is made on your turn

**Nothing is decided while a round resolves.** There are no activated
abilities, no targeting prompts, no reactions and **no randomness anywhere in
how a round plays out**. Every attack, heal, generation and keyword is fully
defined by the board at the moment combat begins, and given that board the
whole round is a function.

What the player chooses, all of it on their own turn:

1. **which card to play**,
2. **front column or rear column**,
3. **which two of their own cells to swap** (twice a turn), and
4. **each ranged character's STANCE** — FRONT or SNIPE (§5.4).

**A stance is a standing order, not a decision taken during combat**, which is
why it belongs on that list without weakening anything above it: it is set on
your turn, it is visible on the board to both players, and it is as much a part
of the position as which column a character is standing in. It is a placement
choice pointed forward instead of sideways.

That is the entire game, and everything below is written to keep it that way.

Four things fall straight out of it, and all four are wins:

- **A turn is fast**, which matters on a machine where a click costs a repaint.
- **The AI cannot be wrong about the rules**, because the rules have no
  branches it has to guess at (§10.3).
- **The network protocol is four message types** (§12.3), and a board checksum
  either matches or does not.
- **`duelsim.py` can play millions of matches** (§13.1), because a match is a
  pure function of two decks and their draw order.

**The one place randomness remains is the shuffle** (§6.1). §17 question 1 asks
whether even that should go.

### 5.1 Resources

| | earned | spent on |
|---|---|---|
| **GOLD** | a base income each round, plus every surviving character's `GOLD` action | most characters |
| **SOULS** | when an enemy dies: **the killer's owner gains souls equal to the victim's POWER** | the expensive and the faction-defining cards — and most of **THE EMBER CHOIR** |

Both **accumulate** — a pool, not a per-turn allowance — and the HUD shows the
pool and the income separately, exactly as the reference does (`4 gold`,
`INCOME THIS TURN 4`).

**The pair is self-balancing.** Gold rewards investment and a held line; souls
reward winning fights. A turtle accumulates gold and cannot reach souls; an
aggressor accumulates souls and starves for gold.

**POWER** is an authored field per card, roughly its total cost, and it is what a
kill pays. Killing a 7-cost character pays seven souls; killing a 1-cost body
pays one. **Trading down is punished automatically.**

### 5.2 A character — and two stat blocks that may be different ROLES

| stat | range | notes |
|---|---|---|
| `MELEE` | 0–6 | may be 0. Reaches only from your **front** column |
| `RANGED` | 0–5 | may be 0. Reaches from **either** column |
| `HP` | 1–12 | current and max; HP damage **persists between rounds** |
| `SHIELD` | 0–4 | **refills to max at the start of every round.** Absorbs that much damage, and **blocks enemy ranged fire from passing into the cell behind it** |
| `GOLD` | 0–4 | added in spoils if the character survived |
| `HEAL` | 0–6 | a healing **pool** spent down a fixed chain (§5.5) |
| `POWER` | 1–10 | what a kill of it is worth in souls |
| keywords | 0..n | all passive, all automatic (§7) |

**Every card carries TWO stat blocks — FRONT and REAR — and they may be
completely different roles.** This is the mechanical heart of the game.

```
  THE STEADING                      3 gold                   POWER 3
  FRONT   melee 3  ranged 0  hp 5  shield 2  gold 0  heal 0
          a shield on one arm and a short sword on the other
  REAR    melee 0  ranged 1  hp 5  shield 0  gold 2  heal 0
          the same man with a pitchfork, working the ground behind the line
```

HP and POWER are normally the same in both blocks. Everything else may change,
including what the character *is*. **The art carries that** (§4.2.1): the same
face, the same build, a different stance and a different tool.

**And no card is a single number.** A melee specialist carries a second stat —
a point of shield, a point of gold in the rear, one point of ranged — because a
board of pure specialists resolves the same way every time and a board of mixed
ones does not. §7.1 makes that a rule the card gate checks.

### 5.3 THE ACTION — one concept, and where each kind resolves

**Every character has exactly one ACTION a round**, and which phase it resolves
in follows from what the action is:

| action | resolves in |
|---|---|
| **attack** (melee and/or ranged) | the **combat** phase, on the character's **own lane** (§6.4) |
| **heal** | the **combat** phase, on the character's **own lane**, immediately after that lane's attacks (§5.5) |
| **generate gold** | the **spoils** phase (§6.6) |
| **a keyword** | whichever phase the keyword names |

A character with both `MELEE` and `RANGED` makes **both** attacks — one action,
not two. A character with an attack *and* a `HEAL` does both, in that order,
which is what makes §7.4's shielded front healer work.

**A CHARACTER PLAYED THIS TURN TAKES ITS ACTION THIS ROUND.** There is no
summoning sickness. A shooter played into the rear shoots in this round's
combat; a generator played into the rear pays this round's spoils.

That is a **large tempo statement** and it shapes the whole cost curve:
resources convert to effect with no delay, so a round is dense, HP totals need
to be higher, and a big turn really swings. §13.2 makes it dial #1, because if
matches come out too short this is the lever — not the card numbers.

### 5.4 Targeting — determined by the board, and the board includes a stance

**MELEE** — an attacker in your **front** column, lane R. No choice at all:

1. Enemy **front** column, lane R — if occupied, hit it.
2. Else enemy **rear** column, lane R — if occupied, hit it. *(The gap punish:
   an empty front cell lets melee through to whatever is behind it.)*
3. Else the **enemy player**.

A melee character in your **rear** column cannot attack at all — which is why
its rear block is usually a different job.

**RANGED** — an attacker in **either** of your columns, lane R, shooting
according to **the stance you set on your turn**:

**STANCE: FRONT** *(the default)* — take the nearest thing in the lane.

1. Enemy **front** cell — if occupied, hit it.
2. Else enemy **rear** cell — if occupied, hit it.
3. Else the **enemy player**.

**STANCE: SNIPE** — shoot past the line at the support behind it.

1. The enemy **rear** cell in lane R, **if the line of fire is open** — which it
   is when the enemy front cell is empty, or holds a character whose shield is
   **0 after this shooter's `PIERCE`** (§7.3).
2. If the rear cell is empty, the shot falls through to the **enemy player**.
3. **If the lane is walled** — a shield the shooter cannot pierce — the stance
   **falls back to FRONT** for that shot and hits the wall. A rule, not a
   choice: you set SNIPE, your opponent walls the lane after you, and the arrow
   goes into the shield rather than nowhere.

**THE REAR BONUS: +1 damage** when the shooter is in **your rear column** and
its target is in **their front column** — so a rear archer on FRONT stance gets
it and a rear archer that successfully SNIPEs does not.

**That is the trade the stance exists for**, and it is a good one: from the back
column you choose between *+1 into the wall* and *full damage onto the
generators and healers behind it*. `PIERCE` is what makes the second option
available at all, and the rear bonus is what stops the first from being a
consolation prize.

**This is where the shield's second job lives**, and it is the rule most likely
to be forgotten: **a shielded front character protects the character behind it
from arrows, and an unshielded one does not.**

**Setting a stance is free and unlimited** during your turn (§6.3). **SNIPE is
greyed** on a character whose lane is currently walled — §47's rule, and the
wall is a *fact* rather than a guess, so it greys rather than failing silently.
Greying it does not make it unsettable later: a stance already set survives the
lane being walled and falls back per rule 3.

### 5.5 HEALING — a pool down a fixed chain, fired on the healer's own lane

**A healer has total healing, not area healing.** `HEAL n` is a pool of `n`
points spent down a fixed priority chain; it stops when the pool is empty or the
chain runs out.

#### 5.5.1 The chain

Let `f` be **your own other column** — the front column if the healer is in the
rear, the rear column if the healer is in the front. **`f` is never an enemy
cell, so a healer can never heal an enemy.** For a healer at (c, r):

| # | cell | |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `(c, r)` | **itself** |
| 2 | `(f, r)` | across, same lane |
| 3 | `(c, r-1)` | above, beside |
| 4 | `(c, r+1)` | below, beside |
| 5 | `(f, r-1)` | diagonal up |
| 6 | `(f, r+1)` | diagonal down |

At each step: skip the cell if it is off the board, empty, or holding a
character at full HP. Otherwise heal it by `min(pool remaining, its missing HP)`
and subtract.

**Pointing the chain at your own other column is what makes a front healer a
real character**, and it makes the two placements genuinely different jobs
rather than one job with fewer targets:

- **A rear healer is a medic.** Its chain points *forward*, into the front line
  — where the damage is. It is safe and it repairs other people.
- **A front healer is a sustain tank.** Its chain points *backward*, into the
  rear — where there is usually nothing to repair. But **it heals itself first**,
  and it is a body standing in melee. With a shield on the same stat block it is
  a character that refills its own HP every round while it blocks a lane, and
  that is a different and interesting thing to build a deck around. **THE
  BULWARK** is where it belongs (§7.2).

No special case is needed for either. One rule, two behaviours, decided by the
column — which is the front/rear stat block doing exactly its job.

#### 5.5.2 It fires on the healer's own lane, after that lane's attacks

**Healing is not an end-of-round action. It is part of the combat phase**, and
a healer *fires* the way a shooter does: when its own lane resolves.

Within lane R: **attacks resolve first, then healing.** So a healer in lane R:

| it reaches | and finds |
|---|---|
| **its own lane** (chain steps 1, 2) | damage taken **moments ago**, in this lane's attacks. Repaired |
| **the lane above** (steps 3, 5) | that lane resolved earlier this round, so its damage is **this round's**. Repaired |
| **the lane below** (steps 4, 6) | that lane has **not fought yet**, so anything there is **last round's** leftover damage |

**So a healer's LANE is a third placement dimension**, on top of front/rear. A
healer high on the board is nearly wasted — lane 0 has no lane above it, so four
of its six chain steps are either off-board or speculative. A healer low on the
board catches its own lane *and* the lane above, both freshly damaged.

And because placement fills the **topmost empty cell** (§5.6), a healer you play
arrives **high**, where it is worth least. **Moving it down costs a swap.** That
is a real, recurring, entirely deterministic decision, and it is the best
argument in the design for why two swaps a turn is the right number.

#### 5.5.3 Healing can pull a character back from zero

A character at 0 HP is **marked** dead but is not removed until the casualty
phase (§5.7). **Healing it above 0 clears the mark and it lives** — and it still
dealt its own damage this round, because simultaneity is total.

That is not a special case; it is what falls out of healing firing inside the
combat phase. What decides whether a healer *can* save somebody is **position**:
it must be in the same lane or the lane below, and within the chain. A healer in
lane 4 can save a character in lane 3 or 4; a healer in lane 0 can save only its
own lane. **Nothing about it is chance.**

**Healing is what makes HP damage matter.** Shields refill every round for free,
so shield damage is already temporary; HP damage is the permanent kind, and a
faction that repairs it can grind where no other can. That is THE COVENANT's
identity and its most sensitive balance number (§13.2).

### 5.6 Placement

**You choose a column, not a cell.** A played character goes into the **topmost
empty cell** of the chosen column.

**Deaths do not compact.** A dead character leaves its cell empty, that hole is
then the topmost empty cell, and it is what fills next. The board develops holes
and the holes are where your reinforcements land — rarely where you want them.

**SWAPS — two a turn, four characters moved.** A swap exchanges the contents of
**any two of your own cells**, and an **empty cell is legal content**, so a plain
move is a swap with a hole and costs one of the two.

A swap across columns **changes which stat block is live**, which after §5.2 may
mean changing what the character *is*: the shielded swordsman becomes the farmer
with the pitchfork, and the sprite changes with him. A swap along a column moves
a healer to a lane where it is worth more (§5.5.2).

**Swaps are the player's only control over a board they could not place
precisely** — a stance re-aims a character, but only a swap moves one — so with
activated abilities gone (§5.0) they are where most in-turn skill sits. A swap
is **free in resources**, instantaneous, and commits immediately; the limit of
two is the cost, and a swap-back costs the second one.

### 5.7 Death, souls and the player

- Damage is applied to **SHIELD first**, then HP. Shield refills to max at the
  **start of every round**, so chip damage into a shield is wasted and a burst
  that exceeds it is not. **This is the single most important balance dial in the
  game** (§13.2), and it is what makes `PIERCE` and big single hits matter.
- A character at **0 HP is marked**, and dies in the casualty phase **after the
  whole combat phase has resolved** — so it still deals its own damage this
  round, and a healer that fires later in the phase can still save it (§5.5.3).
- **The killer's owner gains souls = the victim's POWER.** Where two attacks from
  different sources kill one character, the award goes to whichever resolved
  first in lane order; a simultaneous kill inside one lane splits down, remainder
  to the defender. *(Pinned so it is never a surprise — and, per §5.0, it is a
  rule rather than a roll.)*
- **Player HP**: 20 to start. A player loses HP only from damage no character in
  that lane absorbed. **Direct damage to a player is the win condition and the
  only clock in the game.**
- A player at **0 HP loses**. Both at 0 in one combat phase is a **draw** —
  combat is simultaneous, and a tiebreak would be a rule the fiction does not
  have.

### 5.8 The clock

**No turn timer and no deck-out.** A 4.77 MHz machine is not a place for a shot
clock, and a network turn timer would have to be agreed across a 3,741-byte
cable. The clock is the **empty lane**: a board that deadlocks in every lane
generates no damage and no souls, base income keeps both players playing, and a
stalled board is broken by whoever builds the better economy. If playtesting
finds real deadlocks the valve is a **round counter that raises base income** —
a one-line change, deliberately not in revision 3.

## 6. The phases — and every option, at every point

A **ROUND** is: upkeep, the two turns, combat, casualties, spoils, victory check.
A **MATCH** is rounds until somebody is at zero.

### 6.1 MATCH SETUP (once)

1. Both decks are validated (§9.3) and shuffled.
2. **Who takes the first turn of round 1 is chosen at random.** In a network
   match the seed is exchanged once (§12.3) so both machines agree; otherwise
   `OSAPI_RAND` seeded from `OSAPI_GET_TICKS`.
3. Both players draw an opening hand of **4**.
4. Both players start at **20 HP**, **4 gold**, **0 souls**.

**There is no resource compensation, and §6.3.1 is why there does not need to
be.**

### 6.2 PHASE 1 — UPKEEP (automatic, ~0.5 s, animated)

No input. In order:

1. Every character's **SHIELD refills to its maximum** — animated as a brief
   ring, which is cheap and makes the mechanic visible.
2. **Upkeep keywords** fire (`VIGIL`, §7.4).
3. Each player **draws 1 card**. Hand limit **7**; a draw over the limit is
   discarded face-up with a toast. An empty deck draws nothing and is not a loss
   (§5.8).
4. **Base income is paid**: +2 gold each.

### 6.3 PHASES 2 and 3 — THE TURNS (interactive, untimed)

**The turn order ALTERNATES every round.** Round 1 is decided at setup; from
round 2 on, whoever went second last round goes first.

The non-active player's board is fully visible and their hand is not.
**Everything below is available at every moment of your turn, in any order, any
number of times unless a limit is stated.**

| action | cost | limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Play a character** to FRONT or REAR | the card's gold and/or souls | while resources and cells last |
| **Swap** two of your cells | free | **2 a turn** |
| **Set a ranged character's STANCE** (Front / Snipe) | free | unlimited |
| **Inspect** any card or character | free | unlimited |
| **Read the log** | free | unlimited |
| **Concede** | — | ends the match |
| **End Turn** | — | once, and it is the only way out |

**That is the whole list**, and it is short because §5.0 made it short. There is
no ability to fire and nothing to target mid-combat; the stance is a standing
order you set now and both players can see.

**Playing a character.** Pick the card in the right-hand panel, then pick
**FRONT** or **REAR**. The destination cell — the topmost empty one in that
column — highlights before you commit, and **the stat block and the pose art
that will be live there are both shown**, so the decision is made against the
character you will actually get. **Refusals are stated, not silent** (§47):

- *not enough gold* / *not enough souls* — the card greys in the panel with the
  shortfall in its corner;
- *that column is full* — the column button greys;
- *both columns full* — the card greys.

**The character acts this round** (§5.3), and the preview says which action it
will take and, for a healer, which cells its chain will reach — because that is
the information the decision needs, and it is knowable, being deterministic.

**Swapping.** Click a cell, then a second cell of yours. Either may be empty. The
counter reads **SWAPS 2/2** and counts down; at 0 the cells stop responding to
the swap gesture and the counter greys. **A swap commits immediately and cannot
be undone.**

**Stance.** A ranged character wears a small arrow badge — forward for FRONT,
angled for SNIPE. Clicking it toggles, free and as often as you like. It is
greyed while the lane is walled (§5.4), and the preview shows which cell the
shot will land in, because that is knowable.

**Inspect.** Hovering a card in the panel expands it, plays its idle animation
(the 23rd animated feature) and shows both stat blocks with both poses.
Right-clicking anything on the board opens its full card —
`OSAPI_WM_ONRCLICK` is the slot, and it never competes with the left-click
actions.

**End Turn** is a button in the panel, and it **confirms when you still have
resources you could have spent** — one alert, and a setting to stop asking.

#### 6.3.1 Why alternating removes the advantage — and the compensation with it

Revision 2 found that in this structure the **second** player has the advantage:
both players' characters act in the same combat phase, so acting first costs no
tempo and only gives information away. The second player sees the first player's
placement before committing and pays nothing for it.

**Alternating the order each round cancels it exactly**, and the "you would move
twice in a row" objection does not apply here — which it would in a game that
resolved a turn when it was taken. Over a round pair:

| | round N | round N+1 |
|---|---|---|
| player A | first — **gives** information | second — **takes** information |
| player B | second — **takes** information | first — **gives** information |

Player B does take two consecutive *turns* across the round boundary, but the
**combat, casualty and spoils phases run between them**, both players watched
them, and the outcome was fully determined before either turn (§5.0). So the
pair is a straight swap of the same advantage, and player A gets the identical
pair at the next boundary.

**So there is no resource compensation at all**, which is one fewer number to
tune and one fewer thing for a card to be balanced against.

**What is left is parity**, and it is small: whoever takes the *last* turn before
the killing combat has an edge, and which player that is depends on the round the
match ends on. `duelsim.py` reports win rate by *who took round 1's first turn*
(§13.1); if it is not 50% ± 2, the valve is a small one-off sweetener in round 1,
and §13.2 keeps it as a dial rather than building it now.

### 6.4 PHASE 4 — COMBAT (animated, ≤1 s a lane)

No input except **skip** (any key or click, when `Fast Combat` is off). Lanes 0
to 4, in order. For each lane:

1. Compute **both** sides' attacks from the state at the start of this lane.
2. Play the attack animation: melee lunges, shots fly, impacts land, damage
   numbers rise.
3. Apply all of that lane's damage **together**. Shield first, then HP; a
   character at 0 is **marked**, not removed.
4. **Then every healer in this lane fires** (§5.5.2), in column order, each
   spending its pool down the chain. Healed characters flash; a character healed
   off 0 has its mark cleared and is visibly pulled back.

**Attacks first, then healing** — and §13.2 keeps the order as a dial, because
the alternative reading (heal first, so a lane's healer pre-loads it against the
damage about to land) is a different and defensible game.

### 6.5 PHASE 5 — CASUALTIES (animated, ~1 s)

Every character still marked at 0 HP dies together. Each plays a death
animation, its cell empties, and **the soul award floats to the killer's HUD** —
the moment the economy is legible, so it is worth the second.

**On-death keywords resolve here**, in lane order then column order. Anything a
death creates arrives *after* all deaths are resolved, so no chain can run inside
one casualty phase.

### 6.6 PHASE 6 — SPOILS (animated, in sequence)

The brief: *"at the end of round, when characters are taking their end of round
action, they can play in sequence."* So they do — one at a time, top-left to
bottom-right, each with a small animation, and the wheel's whole credit goes to
whichever is acting.

Per player, the round's first player first:

1. **Gold generation** — each surviving character's `GOLD`, with a coin
   animation and a running total in the HUD.
2. **End-of-round keywords** — `LEVY`, `PYRE` and the rest.
3. The **income figure** for the next round is banked and shown.

**A generator must survive combat to pay.** Spoils are after casualties,
deliberately — it puts economy pieces under real pressure and makes killing them
a plan rather than a side effect. A generator that was pulled back from 0 by a
healer (§5.5.3) pays in full, which is exactly the sort of thing a Covenant
player should be building toward.

### 6.7 PHASE 7 — VICTORY CHECK

A player at 0 HP has lost; both at 0 is a draw. Otherwise the round counter
advances, **the turn order flips**, and §6.2 runs again.

**Victory screen**: result, round count, damage dealt, souls earned, characters
killed, lost and *saved* — and in campaign, the **reward** (§11.3).

### 6.8 OUT-OF-TURN, and THE LOG

The waiting player may do **nothing that changes state** and everything that does
not: inspect anything on the board, read their own hand, read the log, concede,
and in a network match see a **turn indicator** and a **link state** badge.

There are no instants, interrupts or reactions — §5.0, and it is also what keeps
the network protocol a turn-swap rather than a synchronisation problem on a
3,741 byte/second cable.

**The log** is a scrollable list, one line an event, cleared per match.
`os88ui.inc`'s scroll bar is the control. It is cheap, it is what a turn-based
game is debugged with, and in a network match it is what tells a player why
something they did not see happened.

### 6.9 SETTINGS

Per-machine, in `SYSTEM/APPDATA/TITHE.CFG`:

| setting | arms | default |
|---|---|---|
| **Detail** | Flat / Banded / Rich | from `OSAPI_CPU_INFO` and the adapter |
| **Fast Combat** | Off / On | Off |
| **Animations** | Full / Reduced / Off | Full |
| **Confirm End Turn** | On / Off | On |
| **Sound** | On / Off | On |
| **AI Difficulty** | Novice / Adept / Archon | Adept |

**Animations: Off** is not cosmetic — it is the honest answer for a machine that
cannot hold the rate, and it is what makes the game playable on whatever the
slowest supported configuration turns out to be.

## 7. The factions

### 7.1 Every faction is a full set — the 16-card template

**Binding, and it is the owner's rule**: *each faction is a full set of character
cards — ranged, generation, shielding and melee specialists, or combinations —
balanced out.* A faction's focus is where its **best** cards and its **keywords**
live, never the only thing it can do.

Every faction fills the same sixteen slots:

| slots | role | cost tiers |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | **melee** specialists | 1, 3, 4 |
| 3 | **ranged** specialists | 2, 3, 4 |
| 3 | **shield / tank** | 2, 3, 5 |
| 3 | **generation** | 1, 2, 4 |
| 4 | **identity** — where the keywords live, and hybrids | 3, 3, 5, 6+ |

A faction weak in a role gets *worse numbers* there, not fewer cards — THE EMBER
CHOIR still has three melee specialists, and they are the worst three in the
game. That is what *"they still need a set of opening moves"* means in practice,
and it is why no faction can be locked out of a line of play.

**And no card is a single number.** Every card carries at least one meaningful
stat outside its role, in at least one of its two blocks — a melee specialist
with a point of shield, an archer that farms in the rear, a generator that can
hold a lane for a round. `t_tithecards` (§14.3) checks it, because a board of
pure specialists resolves the same way every time and the deterministic
resolution (§5.0) makes that flatness worse, not better.

### 7.2 THE BULWARK — shields, gold, melee

The straightforward faction. Holds the line, out-economies, and wins by being
still standing. **Its weakness is reach**: the game's worst answers to something
it cannot walk to.

| keyword | when | |
|---|---|---|
| **GUARD** | passive | while this is in your front column, the character directly behind it cannot be reached by §5.4's melee gap-punish |
| **BULWARK n** | passive | this character's shield never falls below `n` after a hit |
| **RAMPART** | passive | the friendly characters directly above and below get **+1 shield** |
| **LEVY n** | spoils | **+n gold** (rear only) |

**The front healer lives here** (§5.5.1): a shielded body that refills its own HP
every round while it blocks a lane is a Bulwark idea, not a Covenant one — the
Covenant's healers point forward and repair other people.

First-draft cards — six of sixteen:

| card | cost | POWER | FRONT (M/R/HP/S/G/H) | REAR (M/R/HP/S/G/H) | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tollkeeper** | 1g | 1 | 1/0/2/1/0/0 | 0/0/2/0/1/0 | generation |
| **Shieldbearer** | 2g | 2 | 2/0/4/2/0/0 | 0/1/4/1/0/0 | shield |
| **Hammerhand** | 3g | 3 | 4/0/4/1/0/0 | 0/0/4/0/1/0 | melee |
| **Bowline Sergeant** | 3g | 3 | 2/2/4/1/0/0 | 0/3/4/0/1/0 | ranged |

### 7.3 THE EMBER CHOIR — ranged damage, paid for in souls

Kills at range and charges souls for it. **Its weaknesses are generation and
melee**, and its economy is the game's strangest: it turns its rear column into
*souls* rather than gold, so it is always gold-poor and always one kill from its
next play.

| keyword | when | |
|---|---|---|
| **PYRE n** | spoils | **+n souls** (rear only). The faction's economy, and its most fragile number |
| **PIERCE n** | passive | ignores `n` of the target's shield — **and a front shield of `n` or less does not block this shooter's line of fire**, so SNIPE stays legal through it (§5.4) |
| **VOLLEY** | passive | also deals **1** to the characters in the lanes above and below the target |
| **SCORCH** | passive | damage beyond the target's remaining HP carries into the character behind it in the same lane |
| **KINDLE** | passive | when this character kills, **+1 extra soul** |

**The opening problem, and its answer.** Souls come only from kills, so a
soul-costed faction cannot open. So the Choir's **cheap cards cost gold** and its
**expensive cards cost souls**, and `PYRE` is the bridge: a 1-cost gold acolyte
in the rear pays a soul a round from turn one. The curve is *gold early, souls
late*, and a Choir player who never trades is a Choir player with nothing to
spend.

**`PIERCE` is the faction's positional key** (§5.4): it is not only "more damage
through a shield", it is *permission for SNIPE to stay legal through one* — the
difference between an archer that can reach the generators behind a wall and one
that cannot. That is what makes it worth a card slot, and what makes THE

### 7.4 THE COVENANT — positional specials and healing

Wins by not losing. It repairs the one kind of damage nothing else repairs
(§5.5), and its keywords change *what is about to happen* rather than dealing
damage. **Its weakness is raw output**: the lowest attack numbers in the game and
a need for a long match to convert.

**Every one of its specials is passive and positional**, because §5.0 removed
activated abilities — and that turned out to make the faction better, not worse.
An aura that depends on who is standing above, below or in front of a character
is a *placement* puzzle, which is the game TITHE actually is; an activated

### 7.5 Why the three interlock

| | beats | because |
|---|---|---|
| **EMBER CHOIR** | **BULWARK** | `PIERCE` both walks through shields and **shoots past them** (§5.4), so the gold engines behind the wall are reachable — and every Bulwark body it kills is high-POWER and pays well |
| **BULWARK** | **COVENANT** | melee output exceeds healing throughput — a lane taking 5 a round from a healer restoring 4 is a lane that dies — and Bulwark gold outpaces Covenant utility |
| **COVENANT** | **EMBER CHOIR** | Choir damage is *chip* damage at range, which is exactly what healing erases; Choir bodies are fragile, so `BLESS` and a swap turn a stalled lane over; and every Choir shot that fails to kill has paid for nothing |

**A clean triangle, on purpose**: it gives the campaign somewhere to go (§11.2
gates nodes behind opponents that punish your current faction) and gives
deck-building a reason beyond *play the good cards*.

### 7.6 Card types — and the fork

Revision 3 proposes **characters only**, and §5.0 has made that decision much
firmer than it was: an ORDER played from hand is by definition a resolution-time
choice, which is the thing the whole design now excludes.

- **ORDERS** — one-shot effects from hand. They would give a losing board an
  out, and they would reintroduce exactly the mid-resolution decision §5.0
  removed. **Recommend against**, and §17 question 2 is whether the owner agrees.
- **RELICS** — a permanent attached to one of your characters. Compatible with
  §5.0, since attaching is a placement decision. But a relic is a second object
  on a cell already carrying a sprite, an HP bar, a shield ring, a heal badge and
  a keyword badge on a 104×72 tile. **Recommend against** on §8's budget.

**The remaining thirty cards are wave 10's work.** A card list written before the
balance harness exists is a list of guesses; §13.1 is what has to be standing
first.

## 8. What a cell shows

A character's cell carries a lot in 104×72 (and 80×30 on a CGA), so the budget is
stated here rather than discovered during wave 3:

| element | where | when it redraws |
|---|---|---|
| the **sprite**, in its live pose | the 72×64 band | on its animation clock (§3.6) |
| **HP** | a bar under the sprite, inside the band | with the sprite |
| **SHIELD** | pips along the bar, inside the band | with the sprite |
| **stance** arrow | a corner badge, inside the band — ranged characters only | with the sprite |
| **heal** badge | a corner badge, inside the band — healers only | with the sprite |
| **keyword** badge | a corner badge, inside the band | with the sprite |
| the **cell ground** | the rest of the 104×72 cell | once, with the board |

**Everything that changes is inside the one band**, which is what keeps a cell at
one blit. A badge outside the band would be a second arrival a feature — 23 more
calls a frame, and §1.1's warning about what a call costs applies in the
direction that hurts.

**Three badges is the worst case and it is rare** — a character is a shooter or
a healer, seldom both, and a keyword badge is only drawn where a keyword changes
what the player should expect. Two corners is the design target and the third is
the overflow.

On a CGA the bar and pips survive and the badges collapse to a single **status
dot**, because 80×30 will not carry a figure and three badges. The **stance**
still has to read there, though, since it changes where the arrow goes — so on
CGA the dot is *shaped* by the stance rather than accompanied by it. All of
that is a layout-table decision, not a second code path.

## 9. Deck building

### 9.1 The collection

Cards are **owned**, and ownership is earned in the campaign (§11.3). A fresh
install grants a **starter deck per faction** — the 20 cards that make each
faction playable — so a new player can duel immediately, and the campaign
*widens* rather than unlocks.

### 9.2 The builder

Its own screen, from the main menu and from the pre-match screen. Left: the
collection, filtered by faction, sorted by cost. Right: the deck, grouped by cost
with a **cost curve as a small histogram** — the one piece of UI that teaches
deck-building without a tutorial. Clicking adds, right-clicking removes, hovering
shows the full card with both stat blocks and both poses.

**A second histogram beside it: the role mix** — melee / ranged / shield /
generation / heal — because §7.1's whole point is that a deck can be built along
any of those axes and the player should be able to see which one they have built.

### 9.3 The rules

| | |
|---|---|
| deck size | **exactly 20** |
| faction | **one faction per deck**; no neutral cards in revision 3 |
| copies | **at most 2** of any card |
| decks saved | up to **8**, named |

A deck that does not satisfy these cannot be selected, and the reason is on
screen — *"18 of 20"*, *"3 copies of Hammerhand"* — rather than a refusal at
match start.

**"One faction per deck" is worth defending**: it makes the three identities
legible, it halves the art that must be resident (§1.5), and it lets the
pre-match screen load exactly two faction parts.

## 10. The AI

### 10.1 What it has to be

Enumerate the legal actions, score each, take the best, repeat until nothing is
worth doing, end turn. **On a 4.77 MHz 8088 the whole turn must fit in about a
second of thinking**, or the game feels broken.

### 10.2 The branching, counted

| action | count |
|---|---|
| play a card | hand (≤7) × 2 columns = **≤14** |
| swap | 10 cells choose 2 = **45**, twice |
| set a stance | ≤10 |

**≤114 candidate actions a ply**, and a turn is a handful of plies — affordable
because the evaluation is integer arithmetic over a 20-cell board.

**A stance is the cheapest candidate to evaluate of the three**, because
flipping one changes exactly one attack's target and nothing else on the board
moves. `Archon` (§10.4) considers stances *last*, after plays and swaps, for the
same reason a human does: the stance is only worth deciding once you know who is
standing where.

### 10.3 The evaluation

```
score =   Wp * (my player HP      - their player HP)
        + Wb * (my board POWER    - their board POWER)
        + Wt * (damage I deal next combat - damage they deal)
        + Wk * (POWER I kill next combat  - POWER I lose)
        + Wr * (my gold income    - their gold income)
        + Wh * (healing that will land on damaged characters)
        + Wc * (cards in hand I can afford)
        + Wl * (lanes where I am unopposed)
```

`Wl` and `Wc` are what make it play the actual game: an unopposed lane is direct
damage to a player, and a hand you cannot afford is a mis-spent economy. `Wh`
reads the chain *and the lane ordering* (§5.5.2), so the AI naturally swaps
healers downward — which is the same reasoning a good human uses, arrived at from
the same rule.

**The one-ply lookahead is the combat simulator itself** — the same code that
resolves §6.4, on a scratch copy with no animation. That is the most valuable
structural decision in the AI: **the simulator and the game are one body of
code**, so the AI cannot be wrong about the rules.

**And §5.0 makes the lookahead exact rather than expected.** With no randomness
and no opponent choices inside resolution, "damage I deal next combat" is not an
estimate — it is the answer, computable to the point. An AI that mis-plays is
mis-*valuing*, never mis-predicting, which is a far easier thing to tune.

### 10.4 Difficulty — three honest arms, no cheating

| arm | how |
|---|---|
| **Novice** | `Wt`, `Wk` and `Wl` zeroed — plays for board presence and ignores the fight; and takes the *second* best action 1 time in 3 |
| **Adept** | the full evaluation, one ply |
| **Archon** | the full evaluation, plus it considers its **swaps before its plays** (the stronger ordering, and the one humans miss) and looks one further round ahead on the resource curve |

**A campaign opponent also has a personality** — a weight set and a deck, both in
the same table — so *"the Choir cantor who over-commits"* is data.

### 10.5 Where it runs

**On the worker task**, never in a click handler. A one-second think inside a
`W_ONCLICK` is one second with the gfx lock held, and §7 is unambiguous about
what that costs: the pointer stops, the board stops, the machine reads as frozen.
The worker thinks, posts `OSAPI_WM_WAKE`, and the UI task applies the move on the
wake — `apps/ftpd`'s shape, and the only shape §20.6 rule 7 permits.

**The AI animates while it thinks.** The wheel keeps turning on the UI task, so
an AI turn looks like an opponent taking their time rather than a hang.

## 11. The campaign

### 11.1 The shape

A **map** of nodes joined by paths. Each node is a duel against a named AI
opponent with a fixed deck and personality; beating it opens its successors and
pays a reward. The map is a data file (`MAP.DAT`), so a second campaign is
content and not code.

**Three regions, one per faction's homeland**, roughly 8–10 nodes each, plus a
final node. Starting region chosen by the player.

### 11.2 What a node is

| field | |
|---|---|
| name, portrait, one line of flavour | |
| opponent faction and deck | |
| AI weight set and difficulty | |
| reward | |
| unlocks | the node ids it opens |
| **a modifier** *(optional)* | a rule that applies to this duel only |

**Modifiers are the campaign's whole texture and they are cheap**, because each
one is a flag the rules engine already reads: *"the enemy starts with a free
2-cost character in each front cell"*, *"lane 2 is impassable"*, *"you start with
10 gold and 10 HP"*, *"shields do not regenerate"*, *"healing is halved"*, *"you
get one swap a turn instead of two"*. Twenty nodes with twenty modifiers is a
different game twenty times over for the cost of a table.

### 11.3 Rewards

- a **specific card** — how a faction's identity cards are acquired;
- a **choice of three** — the interesting one, a deck-building decision rather
  than a lottery;
- **gold**, spendable at a **shop node** offering a rotating handful.

**No random packs.** A random pack on a machine with no way to trade is a
frustration generator, and choice-of-three is strictly better design for the same
implementation cost — and it agrees with §5.0's temperament.

### 11.4 The save

`SYSTEM/APPDATA/TITHE.SAV`: a version word, the collection as a count per card
id, the decks, the node states, gold, and a checksum. Written with
`OSAPI_FILE_WRITE` at the end of a duel and at every deck edit — **never
mid-match**, because a file write is UI-task context and seconds of `int 13h`.

A save whose version or checksum does not match is **reported and kept**, not
silently reset. §18.94's discipline: carry the evidence out.

## 12. Multiplayer

### 12.1 The three transports, and their honest status

| transport | status | what it needs |
|---|---|---|
| **hot-seat** — two players, one machine | **free** | a "pass the machine" screen that hides the hand |
| **Ethernet** — two os8088 machines, each with a NIC | **buildable today** | `apps/os88sock.inc` + `drivers/net/netpkg.inc`; `ETHER.DRV` publishes `NETV_LISTEN` and `NETV_ACCEPT` |
| **direct cable** — null modem, serial or parallel | **needs new work, and it is a driver** | §12.5 |

**Hot-seat is not a consolation prize.** It always works, it is how the game gets
playtested, and it is what the balance work is done on. It ships in wave 3.

### 12.2 Ethernet, concretely

`os88sock.inc`'s `net_find` answers which class is serving sockets and sets
`NET_CLASS`; **every verb is non-blocking by contract**, because a blocking
socket call would be a hang with the gfx lock held.

```
HOST:  NETV_LISTEN (CX = port)  -> a listening handle
       NETV_ACCEPT             -> polled from the worker until a handle arrives
JOIN:  NETV_OPEN  (ES:SI = a NUL host name or dotted quad)
       NETV_STATUS             -> polled until NSK_UP
```

**The trap the header states twice**: a zero-length `NETV_RECV` is **not**
end-of-stream. The end is `NSK_CLOSING` with nothing readable. A message arriving
in two writes would otherwise be truncated at the gap.

The socket is driven entirely from the **worker** — which is why every verb is on
§20.6 rule 7's allowlist. The worker stages received messages into a ring in the
package's own segment and posts `OSAPI_WM_WAKE`; the UI task applies them. **The
staging buffer must be in the package's own segment** — §77.10's lesson:
`OSAPI_DRV_CALL` puts the *caller's* segment in ES, so a buffer in a heap claim is
read out of the package image instead, and it presents as memory corruption
rather than as a wrong segment register.

### 12.3 The protocol — four message types, because §5.0 left four

**Action replication, not state replication**: each side owns its own deck, hand
and draw order, and sends only what the other side must know.

```
  HELLO    version, faction, deck hash, a 16-bit seed, player name
  READY    both sides have loaded their faction parts
  PLAY     card id, column           <- 4 bytes
  SWAP     cell a, cell b            <- 3 bytes
  STANCE   cell, stance              <- 3 bytes
  ENDTURN  + a 16-bit CHECKSUM of the whole board state
  CONCEDE
  PING     every ~3 seconds of idle, so a dropped link is noticed
```

**A stance is sent because it is board state**, exactly like a placement — and
it is covered by `ENDTURN`'s checksum for the same reason. There is no `ABILITY`
message and no targeting message, because **the resolution is a pure function of
the board** (§5.0). That is not a small saving: it is the reason a desync can
only come from a genuine rules disagreement, never from two machines making
different choices.

**The checksum on `ENDTURN` is the whole safety net.** Two machines running the
same rules on the same actions must reach the same board; if the checksums differ
the match stops and says **desync** with the round number, rather than quietly
diverging into two different games. In development the mismatching side also
dumps both boards to a file.

**Who takes round 1's first turn is decided by the seeds in `HELLO`**, exchanged
both ways and combined, so neither side can pick. Every round after that
alternates (§6.3) and needs no message at all.

### 12.4 Who does what, and when

Only the player whose turn it is sends actions. The other side **applies them as
they arrive and animates them** — so you watch your opponent play, which is most
of what makes a networked turn-based game feel live. `ENDTURN` hands over.

A link that drops mid-match: the receiving side shows **Link lost** with the
round, and offers **reconnect** (the protocol resumes from the last agreed
`ENDTURN`, because both sides hold a consistent board at that point) and
**concede**. Reconnect is revision 4; revision 3 reports and ends the match.

### 12.5 The direct cable — what it actually costs

**This is the one part of the brief the OS does not already do.**

What exists: `drivers/net/lplink.inc`, a fully-interlocked four-phase nibble
transport over a LapLink parallel cable, **measured at 3,741 bytes/second** on
real hardware, with every deadline counted **in system ticks** because the two
ends are not alike. It is `%include`d by both `NET.DRV` and the field diagnostic,
so there is exactly one implementation.

What does not exist:

1. **An os8088-to-os8088 peer.** `NET.DRV` is written with os8088 as the
   **master** and a *DOS* program as the slave (`NET-PLAN` §1.3), and its socket
   verbs forward to a TCP stack **on the DOS side**. Two os8088 machines have no
   master, no slave, and no stack to forward to.
2. **Any serial transport at all.** The mouse owns a COM port for input; there is
   no UART driver, no framing, no flow control. A serial null modem is a **new
   driver from nothing**, with a hazard the parallel one does not have: port
   arbitration against the mouse is a real design question.

Three candidates, and the plan recommends the third:

- **(a) Extend `NET.DRV` with a peer mode.** Cheapest in new code, worst in
  ownership: a driver whose subject is *"a volume served by a DOS machine"* would
  also own a game's transport, and every user of the cable pays resident bytes.
- **(b) Do it inside the package.** Refused. A package banging parallel ports
  makes two owners of one port, and `DOS-CABLE-NET-PLAN` §2 already refused the
  same shape for the same reason.
- **(c) `LINK.DRV` — a new driver with one subject: a cable between two os8088
  machines.** It `%include`s `lplink.inc` for the parallel arm exactly as the
  diagnostic does, adds a serial arm behind the same framing, and publishes
  `NETV_*`-shaped verbs so **`os88sock.inc` finds it and TITHE's networking code
  does not change one line**. Not mounted by default (`DRVR_WANT` ships 0,
  §51.3) — and §62.1's particular reason applies doubly: finding a cable means
  *writing to ports*, and a port with a printer on it is a real machine.

  Its symmetry problem is already solved by the game: the player who chose
  **Host** is the master and the one who chose **Join** is the slave, decided
  before the driver is asked.

**Staging.** `LINK.DRV` is a wave of its own and **not** in the critical path:
TITHE is complete and shippable with hot-seat, AI, campaign and Ethernet.
Building the cable first would put the hardest and least-testable component — no
emulator here has a null modem between two guests — in front of everything the
player actually sees.

### 12.6 What can and cannot be tested

`docs/TESTING.md`'s list is binding and networking is on it: **MartyPC has no NIC
of any kind**, so every Ethernet row boots QEMU, as `tests/ethernet.py` already
does. A **cable between two guests** is on nobody's list — the parallel link's own
gate is `tests/lptlink/linksim.py`, a host-side model of both ends over one
microsecond clock, and it **caught three timing bugs before any hardware ran**.
`LINK.DRV` gets the same instrument and the same warning: the simulator found the
three a fast host and a slow guest would each have hidden from the other, and the
*field* found six it could not.

## 13. Balance — how it gets iterated

### 13.1 The harness comes before the card list

`tools/duelsim.py` — a **host-side Python reference implementation of the
rules**, in `tools/weavesim.py`'s and `tools/htmsim.py`'s shape. It:

- plays AI-vs-AI matches at thousands a second, with no machine involved;
- reads the **same card table** the package compiles in — the table is a
  generated include and the simulator and the build read one file, so they cannot
  drift;
- reports win rate by faction, by deck, by **who took round 1's first turn**,
  match length distribution, per-card presence in winning decks, and the **role
  mix** of winning decks (§7.1's rule needs a number attached to it);
- is `--selfcheck`ed in the build the way `weavesim.py` is.

**§5.0 makes it far stronger than it would otherwise be.** With no randomness in
resolution, a match is a pure function of *two decks and their draw order*, so
the simulator can sweep every draw order of interest rather than sampling, and a
reported win rate is a count rather than an estimate. A determinism gate falls
out for free: replay the same match twice and the logs must be byte-identical.

**And it is the second reader of the rules**: the assembly and the Python must
agree, and a `soak` row that plays one scripted match on the machine and diffs
the log against the simulator's is what keeps them agreeing.

### 13.2 The dials, in the order to reach for them

1. **Whether a character acts the round it is played** (§5.3). The brief says it
   does, and it is the largest single tempo lever in the game — if matches come
   out too short, this is the dial, not the card numbers.
2. **Shield regeneration.** Full regen every round is a big number. If burst
   dominates, make it partial; if attrition dominates, leave it.
3. **The heal chain** (§5.5) — the pool sizes, and **whether attacks or healing
   go first within a lane** (§6.4). Attacks-first is revision 3's choice and the
   owner's; heal-first is a defensible different game and the dial is one branch.
4. **`PYRE`'s rate.** THE EMBER CHOIR's economy is one keyword, which makes it
   the most fragile number in the design.
5. **The soul award rate** (currently POWER × 1). Halving it slows the whole game.
6. **The rear ranged bonus** (+1). Small-looking, and it is what prices the
   whole FRONT-vs-SNIPE trade (§5.4): raise it and every rear archer shoots the
   wall, lower it and every rear archer snipes. It is the stance's balance, not
   just a number.
7. **Round-1 parity** (§6.3.1). Only if the harness says the round-1 first player
   wins outside 50% ± 2. Alternating should have removed it.
8. **Base income** (+2). Raises the whole curve; reach for it late.
9. **Individual card numbers.** Last, always.

### 13.3 What good looks like

| | target |
|---|---|
| win rate by who took round 1's first turn | 50% ± 2 |
| faction win rates, mirror-excluded | 33% ± 4 each |
| match length | median 9–14 rounds; under 6 or over 25 is a bug |
| cards never played in a winning deck | **zero** |
| every role represented in some winning deck | all five, for every faction |
| **both poses of a card used across winning decks** | **no card whose rear block is never chosen** — §5.2's whole point is that both sides of a card are real |
| stance mix among rear-column shooters | **neither FRONT nor SNIPE above 80%** — if one dominates, §13.2 #6 has the wrong number in it |
| determinism | two replays of one match produce byte-identical logs |

**`tirule.inc` is used by the game, by the AI and by the network checksum, and it

## 16. The refusals, recorded now

- **Any choice taken while a round resolves.** §5.0: no activated abilities, no
  targeting prompts, no reactions, no randomness. A **stance** is not one of
  these — it is set on your turn and is part of the board (§5.4).
- **`gfx_blit4` for anything that animates.** §1.2: 103 ms for one character.
- **A projected isometric with overlapping cells.** §3.2: the shear buys the look
  for nothing, and a band that is not a rectangle is not something `gfx_blit1`
  takes.
- **An `fsx` bracket with a foreign mode.** §1.6.
- **Erase-then-draw.** §3.4: the screen saver shipped it, the field called it a
  strobe, and one opaque band is both cheaper and correct.
- **A badge outside the sprite band.** §8.
- **Resource compensation for turn order.** §6.3.1: alternating removed the
  advantage, so there is nothing to compensate.
- **A healer that can reach an enemy cell.** §5.5.1: the chain points at your own
  other column, which makes it structurally impossible rather than checked.
- **ORDERS played from hand.** §7.6: a resolution-time choice by definition.
- **Random card packs.** §11.3.
- **A turn timer.** §5.8.
- **State replication over the wire.** §12.3.
- **Putting the cable transport in the package, or in `NET.DRV`.** §12.5.
- **Writing the other thirty cards before the harness exists.** §7.6, §13.1.

## 17. Open questions — the owner's to answer

1. **The shuffle is the last randomness in the game** (§5.0, §6.1). Resolution is
   fully deterministic now, but you still draw from a shuffled 20-card deck.
   Should that go too — you build the deck **and its order**, and draw from the
   top? It would make a match a pure function of two decks, which is remarkable
   for a card game. **My recommendation is to keep the shuffle**: it is the only
   variance left, and without it every rematch against the same campaign opponent
   plays out identically.
2. **ORDERS and RELICS** (§7.6): revision 3 recommends against both, ORDERS
   because they are a resolution-time choice and RELICS on the cell budget (§8).
   Agreed?
3. **Mulligan** (§6.1): one free redraw of the opening hand? One line of code, a
   large balance lever — and if question 1 goes the other way it becomes
   meaningless.
4. **Attacks first, then healing within a lane** (§6.4) is adopted on your call
   and kept as a dial (§13.2 #3). The alternative — heal first, so a lane's
   healer pre-loads it against the damage about to land — is worth one sentence
   of opinion now, because the AI's `Wh` term is written differently for each.
5. **Melee into an empty front cell** (§5.4): reaches the rear cell behind it, or
   hits the player? The first makes rear placement dangerous; the second makes an
   empty lane a bigger emergency.
6. **Deck size 20, hand limit 7, player HP 20, two swaps** — starting values only.
   Two swaps now carries more weight than it did, because moving a healer down a
   lane (§5.5.2) is competing for the same budget as flipping a character's pose.
7. **Sound**: PC speaker tones for the UI and combat, and FM stingers when
   `SOUND.DRV` is present? Not in the brief, and it is real work.
8. **CGA** (§1.4, §3.3, §4.2): the card panel plus a per-axis cut makes it fit on
   paper. It needs looking at on a real CGA in wave 5, and §4.2 names the 70KB
   fallback if the cut is not good enough.
9. **Two poses a character doubles the character art** (§4.2.1) — ~110KB
   uncompressed, ~55KB packed, +74KB resident. It fits with 123 clusters spare.
   Confirming you want the art bill before wave 5 draws it twice.
