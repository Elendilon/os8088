| | |

| **the game** | **TITHE** — package stem `TITHE`, file `TITHE.O88` |
| players | 2 — hot-seat, AI, or networked |
| network | two os8088 machines over Ethernet; **and** two machines on a direct null-modem cable (serial or parallel) |

The three factions, and what each is *about*:

| faction | focus | weakness |
|---|---|---|
| **THE BULWARK** | shields, gold generation, melee — the straightforward faction | reach; nothing answers a board it cannot walk to |
| **THE EMBER CHOIR** | ranged damage, paid for in **souls** | gold generation and melee |
| **THE COVENANT** | special abilities and **healing** | raw damage; it wins by not losing |

**Every faction carries a full set of roles** — melee, ranged, shielding and
generation specialists, and combinations — and its focus is where its *best*
cards and its keywords live, never the only thing it can do (§7.1).

## 1. The envelope — the arithmetic everything else obeys

This section is first on purpose. **18 fps with 23 animated features on a
4.77 MHz 8088 is the hardest constraint in the brief, and it decides the art
format, the sprite size and the animation rate.** Everything later is
downstream of it.

### 1.1 The measured constants

| | |
|---|---|
| system tick | **18.2065 Hz** → **54.925 ms** a frame |
| the target machine | IBM PC/XT, **8088 at 4.77 MHz** → ~262,000 cycles a frame |
| any `gfx_*` arrival, small call | **~756 µs** *measured*, and ~20% less since §5.7 — see the warning |
| `OSAPI_GFX_BLIT1`, 128×128 band | **12,588 µs** *measured* (PERFORMANCE.md Set 77) |
| ...so a 1bpp band costs | **6.15 µs a byte**, **0.77 µs a pixel** *derived* |
| `OSAPI_GFX_BLIT4`, VGA planar decoder | **106.9 cycles a pixel** = **22.4 µs a pixel** *measured* |
| `OSAPI_GFX_BLITP`, four planes | *"the bytes and nothing else"* — **estimate ~3.1 µs a pixel**. **NOT MEASURED anywhere in this tree. §3.7 measures it.** |
| RAM `rep stosw` | **1.76 µs a byte** *measured* |
| RAM byte read-modify-write (a 5-instruction loop) | **15.3 µs a byte** *measured* |
| a full task switch | 693 µs *measured* |

> **Do not quote 756 µs as a floor a design must beat.** PERFORMANCE.md Part 2
> says so in as many words: it is the fixed part of a *small* call, `gfx_hline`
> is 678 µs for 6 pixels and 862 for 312, and Set 89 is the worked example of a
> design argued from the aggregate and got wrong. What this plan leans on is
> the **per-byte band cost**, because the renderer is one blit a feature.

### 1.2 The finding that decides the art format

Three ways to put one 72×64 character on a VGA:

| route | bytes moved | cost *(est. from the rates above)* | 23 features, one update each |
|---|---|---|---|
| `GFX_BLIT4` — packed 4bpp, kernel decodes | — | 4,608 px × 22.4 µs = **103.2 ms** | **2.4 seconds.** Unusable. |
| `GFX_BLITP` — 4 planes, pre-composed | 2,304 | **~14.2 ms** | **326 ms** → 3.1 rounds a second |
| `GFX_BLIT1` + pen — 1bpp band, two colours | 576 | **~3.54 ms** | **81.4 ms** → 12.3 rounds a second |

**`gfx_blit4` is out for anything that moves.** One character would be two
whole frames. It is the right slot for a picture drawn once and the wrong one
for animation — §5.4.1.3's own account of why the planar decoder exists says
exactly what it still costs.

**Sixteen colours costs 4× a 1bpp band, and no trick avoids it**: 16 colours
*is* four bits a pixel, and `gfx_blitp` is already a bare `rep movsb` per plane
per row with zero transpose. It is the floor for full colour, and the floor is
four times the one-bit floor.

**So the sprite is a 1bpp band with `OSAPI_GFX_BLIT1_PEN`'s two colours**
(§3.4), and richer arms exist for machines that can pay (§3.4, §6.10).

### 1.3 …and so the animation rate is a budget

Take **40% of the frame** for idle animation (22 ms), leaving 60% for the HUD,
the card panel, input, the AI's background thinking and slack. With 23
features:

| surface | sprite band | bytes | per commit | commits/frame at 40% | **fps a feature** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| VGA fullscreen 640×480 | 72×64 | 576 | 3.54 ms | 6.2 | **4.9** |
| VGA windowed | 56×48 | 336 | 2.07 ms | 10.6 | **8.4** |
| Hercules 720×348 | 80×46 | 460 | 2.83 ms | 7.8 | **6.2** |
| CGA 640×200 | 56×26 | 182 | 1.12 ms | 19.6 | **15.6** |
| *(VGA fullscreen, four planes)* | 72×64 | 2,304 | 14.2 ms | 1.5 | **1.2** |

**A 4-frame ping-pong idle (A B C B) at 4.9 fps is a 0.8-second cycle** — a
slow breath, which is what a crowd of twenty figures should do anyway. The
owner's own instinct and the arithmetic agree.

**A 386 is a different machine and gets a different answer.**
`OSAPI_CPU_INFO` answers `CPU_8086 / CPU_286 / CPU_386` — *a fact the code can
test* rather than a guess about speed (PERFORMANCE.md rule 7) — and §6.10
turns that into a **Detail** setting with a measured default.

### 1.4 The three adapters

| adapter | screen | pixel aspect | what the board gets |
|---|---|---|---|
| VGA | 640×480, 16 colours | square | the design target |
| Hercules | 720×348, 1bpp | ~square | wider, shorter. Workable |
| CGA | **640×200**, 1bpp | **~1 : 2.4** | only possible because the cards moved right (§3.3) |

**CGA's non-square pixel is what saves it.** A 56×26 sprite on a CGA displays
at roughly 56 × 62 *effective* pixels, so a short sprite is not a squashed one
— it is a correctly-proportioned figure drawn on a raster that is short in
rows. `ddlay.inc` (§93.3) already reads the adapter's pixel aspect for exactly
this reason, and the cut is therefore **per axis** (§4.2).

**A 1bpp adapter reads on one bit and the pen is NOT READ there** (§5.4.2.2).
So faction colour is a VGA feature, and the art must read as **silhouette plus
interior line** on the mono adapters — §93.2.1's finding one game along. That
is an art-direction constraint honoured cheapest at the drawing board.

### 1.5 Disk and heap

**The disk is 354 clusters of 1,024 bytes** — a 360KB floppy's real capacity —
so the brief's 350KB is the whole volume bar a rounding. Every package and data
file on a shipped disk is **lz4-compressed** by default (`PKGZ ?= lz4`, §20.13),
so byte counts must be measured against the *compressed* size; pixel art
compresses well and code does not.

| | uncompressed | on disk (est. ×0.6) |
|---|---|---|
| the package primary (`.O88`) | ~52KB | ~32KB |
| character sprites — 48 characters × 4 frames × 576 B | 111KB | ~54KB |
| card faces — 48 × art + frame | ~30KB | ~15KB |
| player bases — 3 factions × 6 frames | 27KB | ~14KB |
| board art — 3 terrains, isometric, static | 60KB | ~25KB |
| menu art, buttons, title | ~40KB | ~20KB |
| campaign map | ~30KB | ~15KB |
| **total** | | **~175KB of 354** |

Half the disk is spare — for more frames, more cards, a fourth faction, or
16-colour static art. **The disk is not the binding constraint. The frame is.**

**Heap**: at most two factions are live in a match, so only their art is
resident. 2 × 16 characters × 4 frames × 576 B = **74KB** of sprites, plus the
board, the HUD, the card panel and the composition scratch. Call it **150KB**
against ~400KB free — and it is comfortable *because* the sprites are 1bpp.
The four-plane variant is 295KB of sprites alone and fits beside nothing.

### 1.6 What TITHE is not

- **Not a `kern_small` package.** It wants ~150KB of heap and a 128KB machine
  has ~50KB. It goes in `$(SMALLOMIT)` with a stated reason, and
  `soak -k 'smallreq'` is the gate that keeps it there (§24.5).
- **Not an `fsx` bracket with a foreign mode.** It is turn-based and wants the
  kernel's cursor, the event ladder and the file API. §11.2's fullscreen
  *surface* — a real window covering the screen — is what fullscreen means
  here, and **windowed and fullscreen are one renderer at two sizes** (§3.1).
  A foreign mode would mean two art pipelines for no gain.

```
        BULWARK side                      EMBER CHOIR side
     rear      front                   front       rear
  +---------+---------+   .   .   +---------+---------+
  | (0,0)   | (1,0)   |           | (2,0)   | (3,0)   |    row 0
  +---------+---------+           +---------+---------+

  ...                                                       rows 2, 3, 4
```

- **4 columns × 5 rows.** Column 0 = P1 rear, 1 = P1 front, 2 = P2 front,

- The players' **bases** sit outside the grid and are two of the 23 animated
  features.
- **Attacks travel straight across a lane**, and only during the combat phase.

## 3. The renderer

### 3.1 One surface, sized from the content box

`DOT DELIRIUM` is the precedent and its three consequences are ours (§93): it
is **bigger on a Hercules than on a CGA**, it draws **one blit an actor a frame
and nothing else**, and it **re-measures the surface** rather than assuming one.

- `OSAPI_WM_PREFER` takes exactly three (w, h) pairs, in VGA / HERC / CGA order.
- `OSAPI_WM_OWNBG` is set — we paint every pixel of our content, so the
  kernel's white fill in front of `W_PAINT` is skipped. Without it every
  repaint flashes white first, which is PERFORMANCE.md rule 2's canonical
  violation.
- `OSAPI_WM_NOANIM` — a zoom-open on a 450-pixel window is a second of nothing.
- Fullscreen is `OSAPI_FULLSCREEN`. §11.2.1 binds the keys: **F** toggles,
  **Esc** leaves. The layout recomputes from the new content box and the sprite
  masters are re-cut; nothing else changes.
- `OSAPI_WM_ONRESIZE` re-runs the layout when the content box changed and we
  did not ask.

**Layout is a table computed once per size change**, never per frame: the cell
origins, the card slots, the HUD rects, the pen table. A resize is not a thing
that happens inside a frame — §93.5's own words.

### 3.2 ISOMETRIC — the shear that costs nothing

**The requirement**: an isometric board, with each character fully inside its
own space and never overlapping another.

Those two together have a clean answer. The cell grid is **sheared**, not
projected:

```
  screen x of cell (c, r) = BX + c * CW
  screen y of cell (c, r) = BY + r * CH - c * RISE
```

- Two cells in the same column are `CH` apart vertically → no overlap.
- Two cells in adjacent columns are `CW` apart horizontally → no overlap.
- **The rectangles tile exactly. Non-overlap is a property of the layout, not
  a promise the art has to keep.**
- `CW` is a **multiple of 8**, so every cell's x satisfies `gfx_blit1`'s one
  alignment rule for free (§5.4.2 refusal 1). `BX` is 8-aligned by §11.94's
  content-origin snap, which is the default.
- A **row** therefore runs up-and-to-the-right across the screen — which is the
  isometric "across" axis, and it is the direction an attack travels. In the
  reference screenshot the units form exactly those up-right lines.

**The isometric read comes from the art, not from the geometry**: the static
board is painted in perspective (the ground plane, the water, the fences, each
player's territory and buildings), each cell carries a baked isometric ground
diamond, characters are drawn three-quarter view with a drop shadow, and the
column rise does the rest.

**This is what makes isometric free.** A true projected isometric would mean
overlapping bounding boxes, a painter's draw order and non-rectangular bands —
and a band that is not a rectangle is not something `gfx_blit1` takes. The
shear buys the look at zero cost to the primitive.

**One consequence for the art**: the four combat columns must sit on **one
shared ground texture**, and the cell pitch must be a whole number of ground
tiles, so that every cell's baked ground is the identical diamond. If ground
varied per cell, a sprite frame would need one baked variant per cell and the
art would multiply by twenty. The fiction already agrees: each player's own
terrain, fence, buildings and base live **behind** their rear column, outside
the grid, exactly as the reference screenshot has them.

#### 3.2.1 Starting geometry

Computed at layout time; these are the values the layout math should produce,
not constants to hard-code.

| surface | HUD | card panel | `CW` × `CH` | `RISE` | board box | sprite band |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| VGA 640×480 full | 36 | 176 | **104 × 72** | 20 | 416 × 420 | **72 × 64** |
| VGA windowed | 28 | 144 | 80 × 56 | 16 | 320 × 328 | 56 × 48 |
| Hercules 720×348 | 32 | 168 | 120 × 52 | 16 | 480 × 308 | 80 × 46 |
| CGA 640×200 | 24 | 160 | 80 × 30 | 8 | 320 × 174 | 56 × 26 |

### 3.3 The card panel is on the right, and that is what rescues CGA

Cards live in a **vertical strip down the right-hand edge**, not along the
bottom. Every row of board height the hand does not take is a row the five
lanes get — and on a 640×200 CGA that is the difference between a board and a
refusal.

The panel holds the hand (up to 7 cards as a vertical list, the moused-over one
expanded and animated — the 23rd feature), the resource readouts, the **swaps
remaining** counter and the **End Turn** button.

The panel is the same shape on all three adapters, which also means one layout
body rather than three.

### 3.4 One blit a feature, and the ground is baked into it

**The rule, and it is the whole renderer**: a character is put down as **one
opaque band that includes its own ground**, so drawing it where it used to be
*is* the erase. There is no instant at which a sprite is off the glass, no
second call to be pre-empted between, and no erase pass at all.

This is §79.5.1's finding and §93.5.1's. The screen saver's first draft blanked
the box and then drew six primitives into it; the field reported *"the fish
flicker"*, because 4.5 ms with the sprite absent is not a brightness dip, it is
a strobe. **We do not get to relearn this.**

**THE BAND IS THE SPRITE'S OWN BOX, NOT THE CELL.** The cell is 104×72 and the
sprite band is 72×64 — the rest of the cell is static ground, drawn once with
the board and never touched again. That is 576 bytes a commit instead of 936,
and it is worth **38% of the animation budget** for one line of layout
arithmetic.

**And a band is cut on the CELL grid, not the byte grid.** `gfx_blit1` only
asks for a multiple of 8, and a band cut there reaches part-way into the next
cell — so whatever is drawn there is composed partly, which on the glass is a
thing that changes shape when a neighbour animates and changes back. §93.5.1
paid for that one; cells are a multiple of 8 wide by construction (§3.2), so
cutting on the cell grid costs nothing.

### 3.5 Colour — the pen, and the strip trick

`OSAPI_GFX_BLIT1_PEN` (slot 0x04A8) says what a set bit and a clear bit become:
**two colours a band, in one pass, for free** — measured at +0.62% on a 128×128
band, a fixed ~78 µs (§5.4.2.2). On a 1bpp adapter the pen is *ignored rather
than refused*, so one body runs everywhere.

Two colours a character is thin. Three arms, in increasing cost:

- **(a) `Flat` — one pen a character.** Each card carries its own (ink, paper)
  pair, so twenty characters are twenty two-tone figures in up to twenty colour
  pairs. Cheapest; reads as a colourful board of flat figures.
- **(b) `Banded` — horizontal strips, one pen each.** The sprite is blitted as
  2–3 stacked bands — head, body, base — each with its own pen. **The bytes are
  identical**; what is added is one arrival per extra strip. At 3 strips and 6
  commits a frame that is ~12 extra arrivals a frame, and §3.7 must price them
  rather than this paragraph. Buys 4–6 colours a character at close to 1bpp
  cost. **This is the interesting one, and it is the likely default on VGA.**
- **(c) `Rich` — four planes** via `gfx_blitp`, 16 colours, 4× the bytes. §1.3
  says ~1.2 fps a feature on an 8088 and perfectly affordable on a 386.

The art pipeline emits all three from one master (§4), so the setting costs
disk and not drawing time.

### 3.6 The pacing wheel — 23 features at a fixed rate

**A fixed budget, not a hope.** The renderer holds the 23 animated features and
a **credit in bytes per frame**, set at layout time from the measured per-byte
cost and the frame share. Each frame:

1. Walk the wheel from where it stopped last frame.
2. For each feature whose animation clock says it is due, commit its band and
   subtract its byte cost from the frame's credit.
3. Stop when the credit is spent. **Remember the position.**

What that buys:

- **A consistent animation speed**, because a feature's clock advances on the
  system tick and not on how many neighbours drew. A feature that misses its
  slot is *late by one frame*, never slowed.
- **Overrun is impossible by construction.** The credit is the contract.
- **No starvation**, because the wheel resumes rather than restarts.
- **Graceful degradation**: on an expensive surface the wheel turns more slowly
  and *every* feature slows together, which an eye forgives. What it removes is
  the failure an eye does not forgive — some characters animating smoothly
  while others visibly hitch.
- **The attack phase takes the whole credit** (§3.6.1), by handing the wheel a
  different budget for the duration.

Idle clocks are **not** free-running per feature: features share a phase table
with a per-feature offset, so twenty characters do not all inhale on the same
tick. Twenty synchronised idles read as one pulsing blob; staggered ones read
as a crowd.

#### 3.6.1 The attack phase

Attacks resolve **simultaneously from both sides**, **≤1 second a row**, and
the animation may focus solely on the attack. So the idle wheel is suspended
and the whole frame budget goes to the row being resolved: at most four
attacking characters, their projectiles, the impacts and the floating damage
numbers. Five rows × ≤18 frames = **≤5 seconds** for a full combat phase, and
**Fast Combat** (§6.10) halves it.

**Simultaneity is a resolution rule, not an animation one.** Both sides' damage
for a row is computed from the state at the *start of that row*, then applied
together — so two characters that would kill each other both die. The animation
plays them together; the arithmetic never interleaves.

Rows resolve **top to bottom**. That is arbitrary and must be pinned anyway,
because splash effects (`VOLLEY`) read the state at the start of **their own
row's** resolution.

### 3.7 WAVE 0 — the bench that decides all of the above

**Nothing in §3.2–§3.6 is settled until this has run**, and it is the first
thing built: a `tests/` package (never shipped, §19.10's rule) that puts up 23
bands of a configurable size and format and reports guest cycles a frame off
MartyPC's own counter.

| row | question |
|---|---|
| `blit1` at each of §3.2.1's four sprite sizes | the per-byte band cost on **this** build — §1.1's 6.15 µs is derived from a measurement of a *128×128* band and must be confirmed at ours |
| `blit1` + pen, same sizes | what the pen's fixed ~78 µs really is at a small band |
| `blit1` × 2 and × 3 strips | §3.5(b)'s extra arrivals, priced rather than estimated |

Measured on **MartyPC** — a cycle-accurate 4.77 MHz 8088, the default

of the tree it was taken on and of no other.

It also settles a question nothing else can: **whether §3.2.1's cell sizes
survive, or have to come down.**

## 4. Assets — one master, three outputs

### 4.1 The pipeline

Art is authored as PNG at **one master size per kind** and a host-side tool,
`tools/os88tithe.py` (in `os88logo.py`'s and `cp437font.py`'s shape), emits the
shipped blobs.

```
art/char/<faction>/<card>_<frame>.png  ->  CHARS.DAT   (sprite bank)
art/card/<faction>/<card>.png          ->  FACES.DAT   (card faces)
art/base/<faction>_<frame>.png         ->  BASES.DAT
art/board/<terrain>.png                ->  BOARD.DAT
art/menu/*.png                         ->  MENU.DAT
art/map/*.png                          ->  MAP.DAT
```

From one master the tool emits, per record: the **1bpp band** (threshold plus
an authored dither for the mono adapters), the **pen pair** or the **strip
table**, and — if `Rich` survives §3.7 — the **four-plane block**.

### 4.2 The cut is PER AXIS, and that is why one master works

Masters are authored at the **VGA-fullscreen** sprite size (72×64 for a
character) and **cut down to the live size at layout time by nearest
neighbour** — §93.5's approach, where a rebuild is "thirty-five images of at
most 32 bytes and happens when the window changes size, which is not a thing
that happens in a frame."

**x and y are cut by different factors**, driven by the adapter's pixel aspect
(§1.4). That is what lets one 72×64 master serve a 56×26 CGA sprite and still
produce a figure with correct proportions on the glass — the 26 rows display
2.4× tall.

§93.5's lesson comes with the technique:

> **A scaled row is a fixed number of bytes whatever the cell is**, so a narrow
> image is a wide one with an empty tail. `dotdel` shipped a cutter that packed
> one byte a row while the composer strode two, and on a CGA every actor was
> noise. One constant, used by the writer and the reader.

**The fallback, named and costed now so it is not a surprise**: if the CGA cut
of a detailed character is unacceptable on a real screen, a hand-tuned small
master set **for the characters only** is 48 × 4 × 182 B = **35KB
uncompressed** (~21KB on disk). That is a second copy of one eighth of the
weight of the art, not "a full second copy of every art", and it is a decision
taken after somebody looks at a CGA rather than before.

### 4.3 Where the bytes live at run time

`apps/os88parts.inc` (§20.12): named, sized parts inside the one `.O88`,
claimed and loaded on demand, compressed on disk (`OP_COMP`) and optionally
lazy (`OP_LAZY`).

| part | flags | when |
|---|---|---|
| `MENU` | `OP_COMP`, `OP_LAZY` | at the front menu; dropped when a match starts |
| `BOARD` | `OP_COMP` | at match start |
| `FACTION` ×3 | `OP_COMP`, `OP_LAZY` | **only the two in play** — the line that makes 400KB work |
| `MAP` | `OP_COMP`, `OP_LAZY` | campaign only |

**Why parts and not sidecar files**: `apps/c64`'s ROMs were a sidecar until
§20.12 wave 6, and *"a copy that took the program and left it behind was a
machine that could not start."* One file is one file.

**The load path is the regression to watch.** PAINT-1BPP-PLAN is the design record and SPEC.md §42.25 the contract; between them they record a
decoder that shipped at 199 cycles a pixel against the packed arm's 49 — the
*load* half was the real cost, not the draw half. §3.7 gives ours a number.

### 4.4 The heap, and the rule that bites

Every claim is born **pinned** (§66). The package's own region and every art
claim must declare `OS88_REGION_MOVABLE` with a relocation **proc**;
`tests/unit/t_movable.py` fails the build otherwise, and the way out is a line
in `tests/movable.txt` with a reason — a list that only turns one way.

**A region declaration alone is inert if the package hires a worker**: §66.6.2's
frame pins the region however it is declared, so the worker needs
`OS88_WORKER_RESTARTABLE` at the spawn. We hire one (§12.3, §9.5), so both are
owed.

Before declaring, ask what segment words we hold that point inside ourselves.
Here that is the **sprite bank index** — if it stores segments, the proc has
real work; if it stores offsets from a base, the proc is a `ret`. **Store
offsets.**

## 5. The rules

### 5.1 Resources

| | earned | spent on |
|---|---|---|
| **GOLD** | a base income each round, plus every surviving character's `GOLD` action | most characters |
| **SOULS** | when an enemy dies: **the killer's owner gains souls equal to the victim's POWER** | the expensive and the faction-defining cards — and most of **THE EMBER CHOIR** |

Both **accumulate** — a pool, not a per-turn allowance — and the HUD shows the
pool and the income separately, exactly as the reference does (`4 gold`,
`INCOME THIS TURN 4`).

**The pair is self-balancing.** Gold rewards investment and a held line; souls
reward winning fights. A turtle accumulates gold and cannot reach souls; an
aggressor accumulates souls and starves for gold.

**POWER** is an authored field per card, roughly its total cost, and it is what
a kill pays. Killing a 7-cost character pays seven souls; killing a 1-cost
body pays one. **Trading down is punished automatically.**

### 5.2 A character

| stat | range | notes |
|---|---|---|
| `MELEE` | 0–6 | may be 0. Reaches only from your **front** column |
| `RANGED` | 0–5 | may be 0. Reaches from **either** column |
| `HP` | 1–12 | current and max; HP damage **persists between rounds** |
| `SHIELD` | 0–4 | **refills to max at the start of every round.** Absorbs that much damage, and **blocks ranged fire from passing into the rear cell behind it** |
| `GOLD` | 0–4 | added at end of round if the character survived |
| `HEAL` | 0–6 | a healing **pool** spent down a fixed chain (§5.5) |
| `POWER` | 1–10 | what a kill of it is worth in souls |
| abilities | 0..n | keywords (§7) and card-specific text |

**Every card carries TWO stat blocks — FRONT and REAR — and the same art.**
This is the mechanical heart of the game: the same card is a different
character depending on where you put it.

```
  EMBERBOW              3 gold                 POWER 3
  FRONT   melee 1  ranged 2  hp 3  shield 0  gold 0  heal 0
  REAR    melee 0  ranged 3  hp 3  shield 0  gold 0  heal 0
          (+1 more against their front column, by the rear bonus)

  QUARRYMAN             2 gold                 POWER 2
  FRONT   melee 2  ranged 0  hp 5  shield 1  gold 0  heal 0
  REAR    melee 0  ranged 0  hp 5  shield 0  gold 2  heal 0
```

HP is normally the same in both blocks; attack, shield, gold and heal are where
the placement decision lives. **Gold is a rear ability that costs you the
attack** — the brief's own rule, and what makes the back column a real choice
rather than a safe one.

### 5.3 THE ACTION — one concept, three phases

**Every character has exactly one ACTION a round**, and which phase it resolves
in follows from what the action is:

| action | resolves in |
|---|---|
| **attack** (melee and/or ranged) | the **combat** phase (§6.4) |
| **generate gold** | the **spoils** phase (§6.6) |
| **heal** | the **spoils** phase (§6.6) |
| **a special** | whichever phase the keyword names |

A character with both `MELEE` and `RANGED` makes **both** attacks — they are one
action, not two.

**A CHARACTER PLAYED THIS TURN TAKES ITS ACTION THIS ROUND.** There is no
summoning sickness. A shooter played into the rear shoots in this round's
combat; a generator played into the rear pays this round's spoils.

That is a **large tempo statement** and it shapes the whole cost curve:
resources convert to effect with no delay, so a round is much denser than in a
game with sickness, HP totals need to be higher, and the swing from a big turn
is real. §13.2 makes it dial #2, because if matches come out too short this is
the lever — not the card numbers.

### 5.4 Targeting

**MELEE** — an attacker in your **front** column, lane R. Automatic, no choice:

1. Enemy **front** column, lane R — if occupied, hit it.
2. Else enemy **rear** column, lane R — if occupied, hit it. *(The gap punish:
   an empty front cell lets melee through to whatever is behind it.)*
3. Else the **enemy player**.

A melee character in your **rear** column cannot attack at all — which is why
its rear block usually says something else.

**RANGED** — an attacker in **either** of your columns, lane R:

- The **enemy front** cell in lane R is always a legal target.
- The **enemy rear** cell in lane R is legal **only if** the enemy front cell in
  that lane is empty **or** the character there has **no shield**. *(A shield is
  a wall; an unshielded body is not.)*
- If both enemy cells in lane R are empty, the shot hits the **enemy player**.
- **THE REAR BONUS: +1 damage** if the shooter is in **your rear column** and
  the target is in **their front column**. Archers belong behind the line.

**STANCE** — by default a ranged character shoots the enemy **front** cell in
its lane. During your turn you may set any ranged character to **SNIPE**, which
targets the enemy **rear** cell instead. Setting a stance is free and
unlimited; it is a decision, not a resource. SNIPE is **greyed** when the lane's
front cell holds a shielded character — §47's rule, and it is a *fact* rather
than a guess, so it greys rather than failing at resolution.

### 5.5 HEALING — a pool down a fixed chain

**A healer has total healing, not area healing.** `HEAL n` is a pool of `n`
points spent down a fixed priority chain, and it stops when the pool is empty
or the chain runs out.

Let `f` be the column **one step toward the enemy** from the healer's own. For
a healer at (c, r), the chain is:

| # | cell | |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `(c, r)` | **itself** |
| 2 | `(f, r)` | in front |
| 3 | `(c, r-1)` | above, beside |
| 4 | `(c, r+1)` | below, beside |
| 5 | `(f, r-1)` | diagonal up |
| 6 | `(f, r+1)` | diagonal down |

At each step: skip the cell if it is off the board, empty, an enemy's, or
holding a character already at full HP. Otherwise heal it by
`min(pool remaining, its missing HP)` and subtract.

**A healer in your FRONT column loses half its chain**, because `f` is then the
*enemy's* front column: steps 2, 5 and 6 are off your own board and are skipped,
leaving self, above and below. **Healers are rear-column pieces**, and that
asymmetry is the front/rear decision doing its job rather than a special case.

**Healing resolves in the spoils phase, after casualties** (§6.6). A dead
character is gone, not hurt, so healing never resurrects. That is pinned here
because the alternative — healing before casualties, so a healer can pull a
character back from zero — is a very different and much swingier game, and it
is §17's question 4.

**Healing is what makes HP damage matter.** Shields refill every round for
free, so shield damage is already temporary; HP damage is the permanent kind,
and a faction that repairs it can grind where no other can. That is THE
COVENANT's whole identity and its single most sensitive balance number.

### 5.6 Placement

**You choose a column, not a cell.** A played character goes into the
**topmost empty cell** of the chosen column.

**Deaths do not compact.** A dead character leaves its cell empty, that hole is
then the topmost empty cell, and it is what fills next. The board develops
holes and the holes are where your reinforcements land — rarely where you want
them.

**SWAPS — two a turn, four characters moved.** A swap exchanges the contents of
**any two of your own cells**, and an **empty cell is legal content**, so a
plain move is a swap with a hole and costs one of the two.

A swap across columns **changes which stat block is live**: an Emberbow moved
front-to-rear stops meleeing and starts shooting for 3 (+1); a Physician moved
rear-to-front loses half its heal chain. **This is the main lever a player has
over a board they could not place precisely**, and it is where most of the
skill sits.

A swap is **free in resources** and instantaneous. The limit of two is the cost.
A swap-back costs the second swap, which is the point.

### 5.7 Death, souls and the player

- Damage is applied to **SHIELD first**, then HP. Shield refills to max at the
  **start of every round**, so chip damage into a shield is wasted and a burst
  that exceeds it is not. **This is the single most important balance dial in
  the game** (§13.2), and it is what makes `PIERCE` and big single hits matter.
- A character at **0 HP dies** in the casualty phase, **after the whole combat
  phase has resolved** — so it still deals its own damage this round.
  Simultaneity is total.
- **The killer's owner gains souls = the victim's POWER.** Where two attacks
  from different sources kill one character, the award goes to whichever
  resolved first in lane order; a simultaneous kill inside one lane splits down,
  remainder to the defender. *(Pinned so it is never a surprise.)*
- **Player HP**: 20 to start. A player loses HP only from damage no character in
  that lane absorbed — melee or a shot into a doubly-empty lane. **Direct damage
  to a player is the win condition and the only clock in the game.**
- A player at **0 HP loses**. Both at 0 in one combat phase is a **draw** —
  combat is simultaneous, and a tiebreak would be a rule the fiction does not
  have.

### 5.8 The clock

**No turn timer and no deck-out.** A 4.77 MHz machine is not a place for a shot
clock, and a network turn timer would have to be agreed across a 3,741-byte
cable. The clock is the **empty lane**: a board that deadlocks in every lane
generates no damage and no souls, base income keeps both players playing, and a
stalled board is broken by whoever builds the better economy. If playtesting
finds real deadlocks the valve is a **round counter that raises base income** —
a one-line change, deliberately not in revision 2.

## 6. The phases — and every option, at every point

A **ROUND** is: upkeep, first player's turn, second player's turn, combat,
casualties, spoils, victory check. A **MATCH** is rounds until somebody is at
zero.

### 6.1 MATCH SETUP (once)

1. Both decks are validated (§9.3) and shuffled.
2. **The first player is chosen at random.** In a network match the seed is
   exchanged once (§12.3) so both machines agree; otherwise `OSAPI_RAND` seeded
   from `OSAPI_GET_TICKS`.
3. Both players draw an opening hand of **4**.
4. **The second player is compensated: +2 gold and +1 soul**, granted before
   their first turn. *(A starting value — and see §6.3.1, which argues the
   compensation may need to go the other way.)*
5. Both players start at **20 HP**, **4 gold**, **0 souls**.

### 6.2 PHASE 1 — UPKEEP (automatic, ~0.5 s, animated)

No input. In order:

1. Every character's **SHIELD refills to its maximum** — animated as a brief
   ring, which is cheap and makes the mechanic visible.
2. Status effects tick (marks expiring, wards, timers).
3. Each player **draws 1 card**. Hand limit **7**; a draw over the limit is
   discarded face-up with a toast. An empty deck draws nothing and is not a
   loss (§5.8).
4. **Base income is paid**: +2 gold each.

### 6.3 PHASES 2 and 3 — THE TURNS (interactive, untimed)

First player, then second player. The non-active player's board is fully
visible and their hand is not. **Everything below is available at every moment
of your turn, in any order, any number of times unless a limit is stated.**

| action | cost | limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Play a character** to FRONT or REAR | the card's gold and/or souls | while resources and cells last |
| **Swap** two of your cells | free | **2 a turn** |
| **Set a ranged character's STANCE** (Front / Snipe) | free | unlimited |
| **Activate a character's ABILITY** | the ability's cost | once per character per round |
| **Inspect** any card or character | free | unlimited |
| **Read the log** | free | unlimited |
| **Concede** | — | ends the match |
| **End Turn** | — | once, and it is the only way out |

**Playing a character.** Pick the card in the right-hand panel, then pick
**FRONT** or **REAR**. The destination cell — the topmost empty one in that
column — highlights before you commit, and **the stat block that will be live
there is shown**, so the front/rear decision is made against numbers rather
than memory. **Refusals are stated, not silent** (§47):

- *not enough gold* / *not enough souls* — the card greys in the panel with the
  shortfall in its corner;
- *that column is full* — the column button greys;
- *both columns full* — the card greys.

**The character acts this round** (§5.3), and the preview says which action it
will take, because that is the information the decision needs.

**Swapping.** Click a cell, then a second cell of yours. Either may be empty.
The counter reads **SWAPS 2/2** and counts down; at 0 the cells stop responding
to the swap gesture and the counter greys. **A swap commits immediately and
cannot be undone.**

**Stance.** A ranged character wears a small arrow badge — forward for FRONT,
angled for SNIPE. Clicking toggles. Greyed when the lane is walled by a shield.

**Abilities.** A character with an ACTIVE ability wears a badge; clicking it
spends the cost and resolves at once. Passive and end-of-round abilities have no
badge and are listed on the inspect card.

**Inspect.** Hovering a card in the panel expands it, plays its idle animation
(the 23rd animated feature) and shows both stat blocks. Right-clicking anything
on the board opens its full card — `OSAPI_WM_ONRCLICK` is the slot, and it is
the right gesture because it never competes with the left-click actions.

**End Turn** is a button in the panel, and it **confirms when you still have
resources you could have spent** — one alert, and a setting to stop asking.

#### 6.3.1 …and the turn order needs measuring, because it may be backwards

**In this structure the SECOND player has the advantage, not the first.** Both
players' characters act in the same combat phase, so acting first costs no
tempo — it only gives information away. The second player sees the first
player's placement before committing to their own, and pays nothing for it.

The brief specifies the opposite compensation, so revision 2 implements the
brief: **second player +2 gold, +1 soul**. But the direction of the advantage is
an **empirical question in this turn structure**, `duelsim.py` reports
first-player win rate directly (§13.1), and **the compensation may end up going
to the first player instead**. Two alternatives are on the table and §17
question 1 is which:

- **(a)** keep a fixed first player and let the harness set the sign and size of
  the compensation;
- **(b)** **alternate the turn order each round** (random for round 1), which
  removes the standing information advantage structurally and leaves only a
  small round-1 compensation to tune.

### 6.4 PHASE 4 — COMBAT (animated, ≤1 s a lane)

No input except **skip** (any key or click, when `Fast Combat` is off). Lanes 0
to 4, in order. For each lane:

1. Compute **both** sides' attacks from the state at the start of this lane.
2. Play the animation: melee lunges, shots fly, impacts land, damage numbers
   rise.
3. Apply all of that lane's damage **together**.

Damage order within a target: **shield, then HP**. A character reduced to 0 HP
is **marked** dead but stays on the board until casualties, so it still deals
its own damage this round.

### 6.5 PHASE 5 — CASUALTIES (animated, ~1 s)

Marked characters die together. Each plays a death animation, its cell empties,
and **the soul award floats to the killer's HUD** — the moment the economy is
legible, so it is worth the second.

**On-death abilities resolve here**, in lane order then column order. Anything
a death creates arrives *after* all deaths are resolved, so no chain can run
inside one casualty phase.

### 6.6 PHASE 6 — SPOILS (animated, in sequence)

The brief: *"at the end of round, when characters are taking their end of round
action, they can play in sequence."* So they do — one at a time, top-left to
bottom-right, each with a small animation, and the wheel's whole credit goes to
whichever is acting.

Per player, first player first:

1. **Gold generation** — each surviving character's `GOLD`, with a coin
   animation and a running total in the HUD.
2. **Healing** — each surviving healer spends its pool down §5.5's chain, with
   the healed characters flashing in order.
3. **End-of-round keyword abilities** (`LEVY`, `PYRE`, decay, and the rest).
4. The **income figure** for the next round is banked and shown.

**A generator or a healer must survive combat to act.** Spoils are after
casualties, deliberately — it puts economy and support pieces under real
pressure and makes killing them a plan rather than a side effect.

### 6.7 PHASE 7 — VICTORY CHECK

A player at 0 HP has lost; both at 0 is a draw. Otherwise the round counter
advances and §6.2 runs again.

**Victory screen**: result, round count, damage dealt, souls earned, characters
killed and lost — and in campaign, the **reward** (§11.3).

### 6.8 OUT-OF-TURN — what the waiting player may do

**Nothing that changes state**, and everything that does not: inspect anything
on the board, read their own hand, read the log, concede, and in a network match
see a **turn indicator** and a **link state** badge.

There are no instants, no interrupts and no reactions. **That is a deliberate
simplification worth naming**: instants would mean the network protocol has to
interleave two players' inputs inside one phase, which turns a simple turn-swap
(§12.3) into a real synchronisation problem on a cable that moves 3,741 bytes a
second.

### 6.9 THE LOG

A scrollable list, one line an event, cleared per match. `os88ui.inc`'s scroll
bar is the control. It is cheap, it is what a turn-based game is debugged with,
and in a network match it is what tells a player why something they did not see
happened.

### 6.10 SETTINGS

Per-machine, in `SYSTEM/APPDATA/TITHE.CFG`:

| setting | arms | default |
|---|---|---|
| **Detail** | Flat / Banded / Rich | from `OSAPI_CPU_INFO` and the adapter |
| **Fast Combat** | Off / On | Off |
| **Animations** | Full / Reduced / Off | Full |
| **Confirm End Turn** | On / Off | On |
| **Sound** | On / Off | On |
| **AI Difficulty** | Novice / Adept / Archon | Adept |

**Animations: Off** is not cosmetic — it is the honest answer for a machine
that cannot hold the rate, and it is what makes the game playable on whatever
the slowest supported configuration turns out to be.

## 7. The factions

### 7.1 Every faction is a full set — the 16-card template

**Binding, and it is the owner's rule**: *each faction is a full set of
character cards — ranged, generation, shielding and melee specialists, or
combinations — balanced out.* A faction's focus is where its **best** cards and
its **keywords** live, never the only thing it can do.

So every faction fills the same sixteen slots:

| slots | role | cost tiers |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | **melee** specialists | 1, 3, 4 |
| 3 | **ranged** specialists | 2, 3, 4 |
| 3 | **shield / tank** | 2, 3, 5 |
| 3 | **generation** | 1, 2, 4 |
| 4 | **identity** — where the keywords live, and hybrids | 3, 3, 5, 6+ |

A faction that is weak in a role gets *worse numbers* in that role, not fewer
cards — THE EMBER CHOIR still has three melee specialists, and they are the
worst three melee specialists in the game. That is what "they still need a set
of opening moves" means in practice, and it is why no faction can be locked out
of a line of play.

### 7.2 THE BULWARK — shields, gold, melee

The straightforward faction. Holds the line, out-economies, and wins by being
still standing. **Its weakness is reach**: it has the game's worst answers to
something it cannot walk to.

| keyword | |
|---|---|
| **GUARD** | while this is in your front column, the character directly behind it cannot be reached by §5.4's melee gap-punish |
| **BULWARK n** | this character's shield never falls below `n` after a hit |
| **RAMPART** | the friendly characters directly above and below this one get **+1 shield** |
| **LEVY n** | end of round: **+n gold** (rear only) |

First-draft cards — six of sixteen, one per role plus identity and finisher:

| **Wall Warden** | 4g | 4 | 2/0/5/3/0/0 **GUARD** | 0/0/5/2/1/0 **RAMPART** | identity |
| **The Great Gate** | 6g 2s | 8 | 1/0/9/4/0/0 **GUARD, BULWARK 2** | 0/0/9/3/2/0 **LEVY 2** | finisher |

### 7.3 THE EMBER CHOIR — ranged damage, paid for in souls

Kills things at range and charges souls for the privilege. **Its weaknesses are
generation and melee**, and its economy is the game's strangest: it converts
its rear column into *souls* rather than gold, so it is always gold-poor and
always one kill away from its next play.

| keyword | |
|---|---|
| **PYRE n** | end of round: **+n souls** (rear only). This is the faction's economy, and its most fragile number |
| **PIERCE n** | ignores `n` of the target's shield — and may **SNIPE** past a shield of `n` or less |
| **VOLLEY** | also deals **1** to the characters in the lanes above and below the target |
| **KINDLE** | when this character kills, **+1 extra soul** |

**The opening problem, and its answer.** Souls come only from kills, so a
soul-costed faction cannot open. So the Choir's **cheap cards cost gold** and
its **expensive cards cost souls**, and `PYRE` is the bridge: a 1-cost gold
acolyte in the rear pays a soul a round from turn one. The faction's curve is
therefore *gold early, souls late*, and a Choir player who never trades is a
Choir player with nothing to spend.

### 7.4 THE COVENANT — special abilities and healing

Wins by not losing. It repairs the one kind of damage nothing else repairs
(§5.5), and its specials are about *changing what is about to happen* rather
than about damage. **Its weakness is raw damage**: it has the game's lowest
attack numbers and it needs a long game to convert.

| keyword | |
|---|---|
| **HEAL n** | the pool and the chain in §5.5. Resolves in spoils |
| **WARD** | *(active, 1 soul)* a friendly character takes no damage from the next single hit this round |
| **INTERCEDE** | when an adjacent friendly character would die, this one dies instead. Once |
| **BLESS n** | *(active, 1 gold)* **+n** to a friendly's melee or ranged until end of round |
| **CLEANSE** | removes `MARK` and every other negative status from a friendly |

| **Physician** | 3g 1s | 4 | 0/0/4/1/0/**2** | 0/0/4/0/0/**4** | identity |
| **The Intercessor** | 5g 3s | 8 | 2/0/7/2/0/**3** **INTERCEDE** | 0/0/7/1/1/**6** **INTERCEDE, CLEANSE** | finisher |

**Every number in §7.2–§7.4 is a first draft** and will be rewritten in wave 10
against §13's harness. What they are for now is to fix the *ranges* and show
each faction's shape; the remaining ten slots a faction are deliberately not
invented yet, for the reason §7.6 gives.

### 7.5 Why the three interlock

| | beats | because |
|---|---|---|
| **EMBER CHOIR** | **BULWARK** | `PIERCE` and burst walk through shields, `SNIPE` kills the gold engines behind them, and every Bulwark body it kills is high-POWER and pays well |
| **BULWARK** | **COVENANT** | melee output exceeds healing throughput — a lane that takes 5 a round from a healer that restores 4 is a lane that dies — and Bulwark's gold outpaces Covenant's utility |
| **COVENANT** | **EMBER CHOIR** | Choir damage is *chip* damage at range, which is exactly what healing erases; and Choir bodies are fragile, so `BLESS` and a swap turn a stalled lane over |

**That is a clean triangle**, and it is on purpose: it gives the campaign
somewhere to go (§11.2 gates nodes behind opponents that punish your current
faction) and it gives deck-building a reason beyond *play the good cards*.

### 7.6 Card types — and the fork

Revision 2 proposes **characters only**. Two additions are on the table and the
owner should decide **before the remaining thirty cards are written**, since
they change the cost curve of every card:

- **ORDERS** — one-shot effects played from hand (burst damage, forcing an
  *enemy's* two cells to swap, stripping a shield, a resurrection). They make
  the game less deterministic and give a losing board an out. They also make the
  AI much harder and the network log much longer.
- **RELICS** — a permanent attached to one of your characters. Cheap to
  implement; they let a faction's identity be *applied* to a character rather
  than printed on it.

**Recommendation: characters in revision 2, ORDERS considered for revision 3,
RELICS probably never** — a relic is a second object on a cell that is already
carrying a sprite, an HP bar, a shield ring, a heal badge and a stance badge on
a 104×72 tile.

**And the remaining thirty cards are wave 10's work, not now.** A card list
written before the balance harness exists is a list of guesses; §13.1 is what
has to be standing first.

## 8. What a cell shows

A character's cell has to carry a lot in 104×72 (and 80×30 on a CGA), so the
budget is stated here rather than discovered during wave 3:

| element | where | when it redraws |
|---|---|---|
| the **sprite** | the 72×64 band | on its animation clock (§3.6) |
| **HP** | a bar under the sprite, inside the band | with the sprite |
| **SHIELD** | pips along the bar, inside the band | with the sprite |
| **stance** arrow | a corner badge, inside the band | with the sprite |
| **heal** badge | a corner badge, inside the band | with the sprite |
| **ability** badge | a corner badge, inside the band | with the sprite |
| the **cell ground** | the rest of the 104×72 cell | once, with the board |

**Everything that changes is inside the one band**, which is what keeps the cell
at one blit. A badge outside the band would be a second arrival a feature — 23
more calls a frame, and §1.1's warning about what a call costs applies in the
direction that hurts.

On a CGA the bar and pips survive and the badges become a single **status dot**,
because 80×30 will not carry four badges and a figure. That is a layout-table
decision, not a second code path.

## 9. Deck building

### 9.1 The collection

Cards are **owned**, and ownership is earned in the campaign (§11.3). A fresh
install grants a **starter deck per faction** — the 20 cards that make each
faction playable — so a new player can duel, against AI or over a network,
immediately, and the campaign *widens* rather than unlocks.

### 9.2 The builder

Its own screen, reachable from the main menu and from the pre-match screen.
Left: the collection, filtered by faction, sorted by cost. Right: the deck,
grouped by cost with a **cost curve as a small histogram** — the one piece of
UI that teaches deck-building without a tutorial. Clicking adds, right-clicking
removes, hovering shows the full card with both stat blocks and its idle
animation.

**A second histogram beside it: the role mix** — melee / ranged / shield /
generation — because §7.1's whole point is that a deck can be built along any
of those axes and the player should be able to see which one they have built.

### 9.3 The rules

| | |
|---|---|
| deck size | **exactly 20** |
| faction | **one faction per deck**; no neutral cards in revision 2 |
| copies | **at most 2** of any card |
| decks saved | up to **8**, named |

A deck that does not satisfy these cannot be selected, and the reason is on
screen — *"18 of 20"*, *"3 copies of Hammerhand"* — rather than a refusal at
match start.

**"One faction per deck" is worth defending**: it is what makes the three
identities legible, it halves the art that must be resident (§1.5), and it is
what lets the pre-match screen load exactly two faction parts.

## 10. The AI

### 10.1 What it has to be

An AI turn is: enumerate the legal actions, score each, take the best, repeat
until nothing is worth doing, then end turn. **On a 4.77 MHz 8088 the whole
turn must fit in about a second of thinking**, or the game feels broken.

### 10.2 The branching, counted

| action | count |
|---|---|
| play a card | hand (≤7) × 2 columns = **≤14** |
| swap | 10 cells choose 2 = **45**, twice |
| stance | ≤10 |

A single ply is **≤70 candidate actions**, and a turn is a handful of plies —
affordable **if the evaluation is cheap**, and it is, because it is integer
arithmetic over a 20-cell board.

### 10.3 The evaluation

```
score =   Wp * (my player HP      - their player HP)
        + Wb * (my board POWER    - their board POWER)
        + Wt * (damage I deal next combat - damage they deal)
        + Wk * (POWER I kill next combat  - POWER I lose)
        + Wr * (my gold income    - their gold income)
        + Wh * (my healing throughput on damaged characters)
        + Wc * (cards in hand I can afford)
        + Wl * (lanes where I am unopposed)
```

**The last two terms are what make it play the actual game**: an unopposed lane
is direct damage to a player, and a hand you cannot afford is a mis-spent
economy. `Wh` is what stops it playing healers into a healthy board.

**The one-ply lookahead is the combat simulator itself** — the same code that
resolves §6.4, run on a scratch copy with no animation. That is the single most
valuable structural decision in the AI: **the simulator and the game are one
body of code**, so the AI cannot be wrong about the rules, which is the failure
mode that makes game AI feel cheap.

### 10.4 Difficulty — three honest arms, no cheating

| arm | how |
|---|---|
| **Novice** | `Wt`, `Wk` and `Wl` zeroed — it plays for board presence and ignores the fight; and it takes the *second* best action 1 time in 3 |
| **Adept** | the full evaluation, one ply |
| **Archon** | the full evaluation, plus it considers its **swaps before its plays** (the stronger ordering, and the one humans miss) and looks one further round ahead on the resource curve |

**A campaign opponent also has a personality** — a weight set and a deck, both
in the same table — so *"the Choir cantor who over-commits"* is data.

### 10.5 Where it runs

**On the worker task**, never in a click handler. A one-second think inside a
`W_ONCLICK` is one second with the gfx lock held, and §7 is unambiguous about
what that costs: the pointer stops, the board stops, the machine reads as
frozen. The worker thinks, posts `OSAPI_WM_WAKE`, and the UI task applies the
move on the wake — `apps/ftpd`'s shape, and the only shape §20.6 rule 7 permits.

**The AI animates while it thinks.** The wheel keeps turning on the UI task, so
an AI turn looks like an opponent taking their time rather than a hang. That
falls straight out of putting the think on the worker, and it is worth stating
because getting it wrong is the difference between a game and a stall.

## 11. The campaign

### 11.1 The shape

A **map** of nodes joined by paths. Each node is a duel against a named AI
opponent with a fixed deck and personality; beating it opens its successors and
pays a reward. The map is a data file (`MAP.DAT`), so a second campaign is
content and not code.

**Three regions, one per faction's homeland**, roughly 8–10 nodes each, plus a
final node. Starting region chosen by the player.

### 11.2 What a node is

| field | |
|---|---|
| name, portrait, one line of flavour | |
| opponent faction and deck | |
| AI weight set and difficulty | |
| reward | |
| unlocks | the node ids it opens |
| **a modifier** *(optional)* | a rule that applies to this duel only |

**Modifiers are the campaign's whole texture and they are cheap**, because each
one is a flag the rules engine already reads: *"the enemy starts with a free
2-cost character in each front cell"*, *"lane 2 is impassable"*, *"you start
with 10 gold and 10 HP"*, *"shields do not regenerate"*, *"healing is halved"*.
Twenty nodes with twenty modifiers is a different game twenty times over for the
cost of a table.

### 11.3 Rewards

- a **specific card** — how a faction's identity cards are acquired;
- a **choice of three** — the interesting one, because it is a deck-building
  decision rather than a lottery;
- **gold**, spendable at a **shop node** offering a rotating handful.

**No random packs.** A random pack on a machine with no way to trade is a
frustration generator, and choice-of-three is strictly better design for the
same implementation cost.

### 11.4 The save

`SYSTEM/APPDATA/TITHE.SAV`: a version word, the collection as a count per card
id, the decks, the node states, gold, and a checksum. Written with
`OSAPI_FILE_WRITE` at the end of a duel and at every deck edit — **never
mid-match**, because a file write is UI-task context and seconds of `int 13h`.

A save whose version or checksum does not match is **reported and kept**, not
silently reset. §18.94's discipline: carry the evidence out.

## 12. Multiplayer

### 12.1 The three transports, and their honest status

| transport | status | what it needs |
|---|---|---|
| **hot-seat** — two players, one machine | **free** | a "pass the machine" screen that hides the hand |
| **Ethernet** — two os8088 machines, each with a NIC | **buildable today** | `apps/os88sock.inc` + `drivers/net/netpkg.inc`; `ETHER.DRV` publishes `NETV_LISTEN` and `NETV_ACCEPT` |
| **direct cable** — null modem, serial or parallel | **needs new work, and it is a driver** | §12.5 |

**Hot-seat is not a consolation prize.** It is the mode that always works, it
is how the game gets playtested, and it is what the balance work is done on. It
ships in wave 3.

### 12.2 Ethernet, concretely

`os88sock.inc`'s `net_find` answers which class is serving sockets and sets
`NET_CLASS`; **every verb is non-blocking by contract**, because a blocking
socket call would be a hang with the gfx lock held.

```
HOST:  NETV_LISTEN (CX = port)  -> a listening handle
       NETV_ACCEPT             -> polled from the worker until a handle arrives
JOIN:  NETV_OPEN  (ES:SI = a NUL host name or dotted quad)
       NETV_STATUS             -> polled until NSK_UP
```

**The trap the header states twice**: a zero-length `NETV_RECV` is **not**
end-of-stream. The end is `NSK_CLOSING` with nothing readable. A message
arriving in two writes would otherwise be truncated at the gap.

The socket is driven entirely from the **worker** — which is why every verb is
on §20.6 rule 7's allowlist. The worker stages received messages into a ring in
the package's own segment and posts `OSAPI_WM_WAKE`; the UI task applies them.
**The staging buffer must be in the package's own segment** — §77.10's lesson:
`OSAPI_DRV_CALL` puts the *caller's* segment in ES, so a buffer in a heap claim
is read out of the package image instead, and it presents as memory corruption
rather than as a wrong segment register.

### 12.3 The protocol

Turn-based, so this is easy and should be kept easy. **Action replication, not
state replication**: each side owns its own deck, hand and RNG, and sends only
what the other side must know.

```
  HELLO    version, faction, deck hash, a 16-bit seed, player name
  READY    both sides have loaded their faction parts
  PLAY     card id, column
  SWAP     cell a, cell b
  STANCE   cell, stance
  ABILITY  cell, ability index, target
  ENDTURN  followed by a 16-bit CHECKSUM of the whole board state
  CONCEDE
  PING     every ~3 seconds of idle, so a dropped link is noticed
```

A `PLAY` is four bytes, so even a 3,741 byte/second cable carries a turn
instantly. **The bandwidth is irrelevant; latency and liveness are what matter.**

**The checksum on `ENDTURN` is the whole safety net.** Two machines running the
same rules on the same actions must reach the same board. If the checksums
differ the match stops and says **desync** with the round number, rather than
quietly diverging into two different games — the failure mode that makes
networked play untrustworthy. In development the mismatching side also dumps
both boards to a file.

**The first player is decided by the seeds in `HELLO`**, exchanged both ways and
combined, so neither side can pick.

### 12.4 Who does what, and when

Only the player whose turn it is sends actions. The other side **applies them
as they arrive and animates them** — so you watch your opponent play, which is
most of what makes a networked turn-based game feel live. `ENDTURN` hands over.

A link that drops mid-match: the receiving side shows **Link lost** with the
round, and offers **reconnect** (the protocol resumes from the last agreed
`ENDTURN`, because both sides hold a consistent board at that point) and
**concede**. Reconnect is revision 3; revision 2 reports and ends the match.

### 12.5 The direct cable — what it actually costs

**This is the one part of the brief the OS does not already do, and it should be
planned honestly rather than assumed.**

What exists: `drivers/net/lplink.inc`, a fully-interlocked four-phase nibble
transport over a LapLink parallel cable, **measured at 3,741 bytes/second** on
real hardware, with every deadline counted **in system ticks** because the two
ends are not alike. It is `%include`d by both `NET.DRV` and the field
diagnostic, so there is exactly one implementation.

What does not exist:

1. **An os8088-to-os8088 peer.** `NET.DRV` is written with os8088 as the
   **master** and a *DOS* program as the slave (`NET-PLAN` §1.3), and its socket
   verbs forward to a TCP stack **on the DOS side**. Two os8088 machines have no
   master, no slave, and no stack to forward to.
2. **Any serial transport at all.** The mouse owns a COM port for input; there
   is no UART driver, no framing, no flow control. A serial null modem is a
   **new driver from nothing**, with a hazard the parallel one does not have:
   port arbitration against the mouse is a real design question, not a detail.

Three candidates, and the plan recommends the third:

- **(a) Extend `NET.DRV` with a peer mode.** Cheapest in new code, worst in
  ownership: it makes a driver whose subject is *"a volume served by a DOS
  machine"* also the owner of a game's transport, and every user of the cable
  pays resident bytes for it.
- **(b) Do it inside the package.** Refused. A package banging parallel ports
  makes two owners of one port, and `DOS-CABLE-NET-PLAN` §2 already refused the
  same shape for the same reason.
- **(c) `LINK.DRV` — a new driver with one subject: a cable between two os8088
  machines.** It `%include`s `lplink.inc` for the parallel arm exactly as the
  diagnostic does, adds a serial arm behind the same framing, and publishes
  `NETV_*`-shaped verbs so **`os88sock.inc` finds it and TITHE's networking code
  does not change one line**. Not mounted by default (`DRVR_WANT` ships 0,
  §51.3) — and §62.1's particular reason applies doubly: finding a cable means
  *writing to ports*, and a port with a printer on it is a real machine.

  Its symmetry problem is already solved by the game: the player who chose
  **Host** is the master and the one who chose **Join** is the slave, decided
  before the driver is asked.

**Staging.** `LINK.DRV` is a wave of its own and it is **not** in the critical
path: TITHE is complete and shippable with hot-seat, AI, campaign and Ethernet.
Building the cable first would put the hardest and least-testable component —
no emulator here has a null modem between two guests — in front of everything
the player actually sees.

### 12.6 What can and cannot be tested

`docs/TESTING.md`'s list is binding and networking is on it: **MartyPC has no
NIC of any kind**, so every Ethernet row boots QEMU, as `tests/ethernet.py`
already does. A **cable between two guests** is on nobody's list — the parallel
link's own gate is `tests/lptlink/linksim.py`, a host-side model of both ends
over one microsecond clock, and it **caught three timing bugs before any
hardware ran**. `LINK.DRV` gets the same instrument and the same warning: the
simulator found the three a fast host and a slow guest would each have hidden
from the other, and the *field* found six it could not.

## 13. Balance — how it gets iterated

**Balance is named in the brief as a large factor, so it gets a mechanism
rather than a promise.**

### 13.1 The harness comes before the card list

`tools/duelsim.py` — a **host-side Python reference implementation of the
rules**, in `tools/weavesim.py`'s and `tools/htmsim.py`'s shape. It:

- plays AI-vs-AI matches at thousands a second, with no machine involved;
- reads the **same card table** the package compiles in — the table is a
  generated include and the simulator and the build read one file, so they
  cannot drift;
- reports win rate by faction, by deck, by first/second player, match length
  distribution, per-card presence in winning decks, and the **role mix** of
  winning decks (§7.1's rule needs a number attached to it);
- is `--selfcheck`ed in the build the way `weavesim.py` is.

**And it is the second reader of the rules**, which is the other half of its
value: the assembly and the Python must agree, and a `soak` row that plays one
scripted match on the machine and diffs the log against the simulator's is what
keeps them agreeing.

### 13.2 The dials, in the order to reach for them

1. **Turn-order compensation** (§6.3.1). Direction *and* size, and the harness
   reports first-player win rate directly. Target 50% ± 2.
2. **Whether a character acts the round it is played** (§5.3). The brief says it
   does, and it is the largest single tempo lever in the game — if matches come
   out too short, this is the dial, not the card numbers.
3. **Shield regeneration.** Full regen every round is a big number. If burst
   dominates, make it partial; if attrition dominates, leave it.
4. **The heal chain** (§5.5) — the pool sizes, and whether healing resolves
   before or after casualties (§17 q4). THE COVENANT is entirely built on this.
5. **`PYRE`'s rate.** THE EMBER CHOIR's economy is one keyword, which makes it
   the most fragile number in the design.
6. **The soul award rate** (currently POWER × 1). Halving it slows the whole
   game.
7. **The rear ranged bonus** (+1). Small, but it is the entire reason the back
   column is not just an economy zone.
8. **Base income** (+2). Raises the whole curve; reach for it late.
9. **Individual card numbers.** Last, always — a card nerfed to fix a systemic
   problem has to be un-nerfed later.

### 13.3 What good looks like

| | target |
|---|---|
| first-player win rate | 50% ± 2 |
| faction win rates, mirror-excluded | 33% ± 4 each |
| match length | median 9–14 rounds; under 6 or over 25 is a bug |
| cards never played in a winning deck | **zero** — a card nobody plays needs changing, not a card that is weak |
| every role represented in some winning deck | all four, for every faction (§7.1's rule, measured) |

## 17. Open questions — the owner's to answer

1. **Turn order** (§6.3.1). In this structure the second player has the
   information advantage and pays nothing for it, so the brief's compensation
   may be pointing the wrong way. Keep a fixed first player and let the harness
   set the sign, or **alternate the order each round**?
2. **ORDERS and RELICS** (§7.6): characters only in revision 2?
3. **Mulligan** (§6.1): one free redraw of the opening hand? One line of code,
   a large balance lever.
4. **Does healing resolve before or after casualties?** (§5.5.) After means it
   repairs survivors; before means a healer can pull a character back from zero.
   The second is far swingier and makes THE COVENANT much stronger.
5. **Melee into an empty front cell** (§5.4): this plan says it reaches the rear
   cell behind it. The alternative is that it hits the player. The first makes
   rear placement dangerous; the second makes an empty lane a bigger emergency.
6. **A front-column healer keeps `HEAL` at all?** §5.5 gives it a 3-step chain
   (self, above, below). The alternative is that healing is rear-only and a
   healer's front block is pure combat — simpler, and a harder placement choice.
7. **`WARD`, `BLESS` and the other actives** are the first things in the game
   that need a *target-picking gesture* during a turn. Worth the UI, or should
   COVENANT's specials all be automatic?
8. **Deck size 20, hand limit 7, player HP 20** — starting values only.
9. **Sound**: PC speaker tones for the UI and combat, and FM stingers when
   `SOUND.DRV` is present? Not in the brief, and it is real work.
10. **CGA** (§1.4, §3.3, §4.2): the card panel plus a per-axis cut makes it fit
    on paper. It needs looking at on a real CGA in wave 5, and §4.2 names the
    35KB fallback if the cut is not good enough.
