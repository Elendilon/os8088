# Bounce Bonanza for os8088

A native 8086 rolling-ball game: a ball you roll directly over an isometric
course that **hardware-scrolls in both directions** on the two 1983 graphics
cards, the CGA (320x200, four colours) and the Hercules (a 360x200 field of
pair pixels). It is this project's own design, not a remake: the ball is
generated by `tools/bonanza_assets.py` from code, the courses are text
(`apps/bonanza/courses/*.bbc`) compiled by `tools/bbcourse.py`, and nothing
else is read at build time or at run time. See SPEC.md 128 and
`docs/plans/BOUNCE-BONANZA-PLAN.md`.

**Status: wave 4, a game.** Six courses - "First Roll", "Bounce Trial",
"Windmill Lane", "Moonlit Green", "Summit Rush" and the benchmark's "Proving
Ground" - with flags, ball-swap pads, springs, tubes, windmills, bumper posts,
a lift and the cup; three balls (rubber, steel, glass); the five-course tour
on G with a scorecard and best times kept on disk; demos saved and loaded on
disk (W, L), benchmarked (D) and shipped (V); the mouse as a tilt stick (T);
and music and impacts (S). There is no in-play HUD (it did not fit; SPEC.md
128.14.16) - the time is on the results face. SPEC.md 128.10 - 128.14 and
128.20 are the contract.

Keys, on the desktop: Enter, F or Alt+Enter plays the selected course with
the selected ball. **N cycles the course** through the five ("First Roll",
"Bounce Trial", "Windmill Lane", "Moonlit Green", "Summit Rush", then "First
Roll" again; each sets the ball to the course's own - the sixth, "Proving
Ground", is the benchmark's, played by B) and **M cycles the ball**
(rubber, steel, glass); the face names both. **B runs the benchmark** on its
own course, "Proving Ground", with that course's ball (the autopilot drives
the course's route for 1,200 frames, then the window shows the frames, the
display rate, the refreshes missed and the worst frame, and the cost of each
part of a frame; the benchmark always runs the GLIDE camera). **C toggles
the camera** between GLIDE and TRICKLE (SPEC.md 128.11.11.1) on the desktop,
where the face names it, and while playing. C, M and N held down count as
ONE press, and every choice lasts until the package closes. **R replays the
last play** - the same steps, on either card - once one has been recorded
(SPEC.md 128.11.12). Any key after a benchmark's results returns the window
to its main face. Playing: the arrows roll
the ball, Space hops, Left Shift grips; Esc, F or Alt+Enter goes back to the
desktop.

**The game** (SPEC.md 128.14.3): **G starts a tour** of the five courses
after the benchmark's, each with its own ball, raced for total time against
each course's par - there are no lives, a fall costs the way back. After each
course the window is the SCORECARD (every course's time, `*` at or under par,
its falls, the par and the best) and Enter plays the next; Esc in a course or
on the scorecard stops the tour. While playing, **P pauses** and Backspace
returns the ball to its last flag. The best course times and the best tour
are kept in `SYSTEM\APPDATA\BONANZA.DAT` on the game's own disk (only a play
started with the course's own ball sets one). After any play or replay the
face shows its time, how it ended, its falls and the flags it reached.

**Demos** (SPEC.md 128.14.7): **W saves** the last play (or the demo held) as
a `.BZR` file through the Save dialog, and **L loads** one through the Open
dialog; a `.BZR` is BONANZA's document, so double-clicking one in a Disk
window opens the game with it. A loaded file is checked in full before it is
used - another build's course, other physics, a damaged or foreign file is
refused and the demo held is kept. **V** takes the next of the five shipped
demos (one a tour course), **R** watches the demo held and **D** times it:
the demo replayed with the benchmark's instruments, its results on the same
page as B's. **T** switches the control between KEYS and MOUSE: with the
mouse the pointer is a tilt stick - its offset from where it was when the
play started (or the ball was last put back) tilts the course that way, a
quarter of the screen being a full tilt - the left button hops and the right
grips; a mouse play's demo records the tilt, so it replays without one.
There is no clock on the glass: the course time, the falls and the flags
reached are on the face after every play (SPEC.md 128.14.16). An on-screen
clock was built and measured, cost the CGA 65 missed frames on `MILL` with
the speaker on, and was taken out (SPEC.md 128.14.5).

**Sound** (SPEC.md 128.20): a title theme plays while the window is open and
each course has its own (the first five planned: *Fairway*, *Bounce Trial*,
*Windmill Lane*, *Moonlit Green*, *Summit Rush*), on the PC speaker or, with
`SOUND.DRV` and an AdLib, on the OPL2. Every impact - a landing, a bounce, a
wall, a bumper, a windmill's knock - sounds different for each ball: rubber
boings, steel clanks, glass tinks (and shatters). **S cycles the sound**:
AUTO (the AdLib where the Control Panel's route allows it, else the speaker),
SPEAKER, EFFECTS ONLY and OFF; the face names the choice and the arm found.
The songs are text (`apps/bonanza/music/*.bmu`) packed by `tools/bbmusic.py`,
which also renders them to WAV on the host:
`python3 tools/bbmusic.py wav -o DIR`.

Needs a CGA or a Hercules card (anything else is refused with the reason)
and kern_big or kern_mega; the 128KB machine's kernel refuses it.

Build:

```
make bonanza        # build/bonanza.o88
make bonanzadisk    # build/bonanza.img, bonanza720.img, bonanza120.img, bonanza360.img
make bonanza-check  # the ball generator's own gate
make bonanza-course-check  # the course compiler's own gate
make bonanza-music-check   # the music packer's own gate
```
